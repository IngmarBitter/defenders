Sherlock Holmes paces up and down the floor, puffing on his pipe, obviously in deep thought.  Suddenly he calls out:  "Watson, my good man!  There are a number of clues in this story at the beginning of the book of Genesis that suggest that parts of it are not really a recounting of the physical beginnings of the world and of us humans."

"Really, sir?", Watson replies in a respectful voice. "Doesn't everyone, including those who doubt its truth, believe that it aims to tell the story of creation and of the origin of humankind?".

"It doesn't matter what others believe, my dear Watson!  What is important here is to get to the truth of the matter.  And that means we must look at every case in a completely fresh and unprejudiced manner, entirely uncontaminated by what others might happen to think.  The great mass of humanity are quite unable to see reality for what it is because they let others decide what they should think and how they should see things.  They virtually see through others' eyes and think through others' minds.  The blind lead the blind and they all fall into the ditch of ignorance and confusion. We must rise above such an abortion of human intelligence." 

"Pray tell, my dear sir, what then are these clues you have found, and where might they lead?"

"Come with me, my good man, and we shall question the experts about a few matters.  We're going to the university."

Sherlock takes a puff on his pipe and then abruptly strides away at a fast pace, Watson rushing to keep up.  After a while the two arrive at the university and head immediately for the Department of Zoology.  Sherlock knocks at the office door of Professor Pontius Mython who is an expert in herpetology, the study of reptiles and amphibians.  In a short while a man with a gray beard and intentionally uncombed hair appears at the door.

"Ah, how good to see you, Sherlock!" exclaims the professor.  "Do come in!"

"Ponty, this is Watson, my assistant.  Watson, this is my good friend Ponty Mython, perhaps the world's greatest expert on snakes."

"I'm delighted to meet you, Mr. Watson.  I must apologize most profusely to the two of you but I must be in a meeting in just 15 minutes.  Is there any way I can help you in just 10 minutes?"

"You can probably answer our question in just a few seconds, Ponty.  All we want to know is the answer to just one question:  Can snakes talk?"

"Ha!", chortled the professor.  "Perhaps in cartoons but not in real life.  They don't have a larynx or voice box and their tongue, mouth and teeth are not at all shaped correctly for them to produce the sounds required in human languages.  They can't do much more than hiss, my good friends."

"Thank you ever so much, dear Ponty!  Well, maybe i have just one more little question.  It will take only a second for you to answer.  Uhhhhh … Ponty, can you think of any reason why a snake in a tree would want a woman to eat the fruit of that tree?"

"Not in this world, Sherlock!  No.  Snakes don't seem to have any real consideration for humans."

"Excellent, dear Ponty!  Perhaps we can discuss such matters at length at another time.  We'll let you prepare for your meeting and be on our way.  Which way is the Botany Department?"

"It's in that building right over there just next to us."

"Superb!  Thank you ever so much, Ponty!  May your meeting be an excellent and fruitful one!"

Sherlock and Watson next make their way to Professor Geistbaum, a man whose life has been devoted to the study of trees.  Quite serendipitously, they meet him just as he is entering his office.

"Professor Geistbaum?" asks Sherlock.

"Yes, yes!  How can i help you?"

"We have just a very brief question for you – a question we know that your great knowledge of trees will surely be able to answer."

"Well, please come into my office and waste no time in letting me know your question."

"Thank you!" replies Watson, now becoming a little more bold and confident.

"Please do tell us, professor", Sherlock queries, "if in your vast experience you have ever come across two trees known as a tree of life and a tree of the knowledge of good and evil?"

"Certainly not!  The names themselves are a giveaway.  The trees i deal with don't have names like that.  Mathematicians deal with ideas like binary trees, and evolutionary theorists have genetic trees and trees of biological descent, which are all just conceptual trees, not physical trees with wood, leaves and the like."

"Just as i suspected", Sherlock blurts out.  "I knew it!  A tree of life and a tree of the knowledge of good and evil are obviously symbolic trees, not physical ones!"

"Ah, i see!" chimes in Watson.  "Yes, now it's clear.  One tree represents a higher kind of human life and the other symbolizes knowing right from wrong.  How simple!  This uses a physical tree to symbolize an idea or principle."

"Well, that makes sense." the professor adds "That's like the way we use the physical heart that pumps blood to represent the heart that feels emotions.  If you suffer from a broken heart, you can't go to the cardiologist for help because that broken heart isn't a physical one!  Ha! Consider how our dreams speak to us in terms of symbols rather than directly conveying something to us.  Sometimes we are not strong enough to deal with painful truths and bitter realities about ourselves.  If told directly and bluntly, we would reject these truths or else become deeply hurt and angry, or perhaps depressed and in despair.  So, our dreams speak to our deeper subconscious parts with symbols, perhaps hoping that these will gradually awaken us to our shortcomings and defects in a gentle and gradual way that is constructive instead of harmful.  Now, if this is how it works in our dreams, then why could not a Higher Power also speak to us in these subtle symbolic ways that speak to our subconscious rather than steamroller us with too much painful truth?"

"That's brilliant, Sherlock!  I mean sir!  Of course!  And this Creator might also want to speak only to some who can grasp these spiritual ideas and leave the less developed minds and hearts with their absurd and unreasonable materialist meanings."

  

"Exactly, my dear Watson!  You are showing the signs of becoming a most insightful investigator of reality.  I do believe that we have just about solved this mystery."

  

"And, sir, if you will permit me to say so, since this Garden of Eden story is so clearly a symbolic allegory rather than an account of actual physical events, and since it is obviously a continuation of and a part of the creation story, then could not we also conclude that this creation story is also allegorical, symbolical, and not strictly the story of how and when the world was physically created?  Furthermore, the symbols and objects of these allegories would have to be in accord with the current understanding of the culture of the time in which this story was written. "Thank you, sir!  How very strange that so many who regard themselves as religious would be so utterly devoid of spiritual insight!"

"Well done, Watson!  I'm quite impressed with you!  You are assuredly a true sleuth for truth!"

STAGES OF HUMAN DEVELOPMENT REFLECTED IN RELIGION

Most of us have heard of the major stages of human development such as childhood, adolescence and maturity.  There are at least four different meanings for these descriptions: childhood, adolescence and maturity.  One definition describes human physical development.   A physical child is clearly different from a physical adolescent or adult.  A second definition is chronological, describing a stage in terms of an age-group such as teenager.   A third definition is cultural.  For example, among the Zulu of southern Africa, a boy, umfana, remains classified this way until he marries – even if he's 40 years old.  Only then is he regarded as a man, indoda, whose opinions are worth listening to.

The fourth definition is a psychological one and describes characteristic traits of attitude and behavior regardless of age, culture or body.  For example, the stage of psychological childhood shows the traits of dependence, mindless imitation, weakness of intellect, inability to separate imaginative fantasy from reality, a selfish orientation to life, and more. An old man can be in this stage of psychological childhood.  Psychological adolescence is typified by a rebellious independence from authority alternating with childlike dependence, an awakening of one's unique self and rational intellect, an impetuousness and instability, and a sense of being all-knowing and invincible alternating sometimes with feelings of being confused and lost.  In psychological maturity one sees the traits of calm wisdom, inner peace, balance and rationality, spiritual insight and development, and the power to override the demands of the lower animal and ego natures that tend to bring conflict, strife and war when they dominate in us.

In the West, adolescence used to be associated with being a teenager, but we see the traits of adolescence in those much older.  In fact, many say that our present-day world is adolescent on the whole though some might also insist that many characteristics of childhood appear too – as when nations go to war and regress to tribal conformity and militancy.  What is certain is that the traits of calm, balanced, wise, fully rational maturity are very rarely seen or heard now in our world (though there are many signs that we are now in that tumultuous stage of transition from our collective adolescence to our general maturity).

These three major stages of development can be broken up into sub-stages.  For example, general psychological childhood starts with infancy and ends in late childhood.  Adolescence has the sub-stages of early and late adolescence, and maturity has its first phase of "coming of age" following by other sub-stages of maturity.

Our general stage of development is reflected in the social order.  For example, the tribal order is ideally suited to a people who are collectively in the stage of psychological childhood, where dependence and conformity are dominant.  The civilization that we've seen over the past 6000 years is suited to collective adolescence with its diversity of occupations, tolerance of "differences", etc.   Of course we haven't yet seen any developed and dominant social structure that is appropriate to our general maturity (though it could now be in its embryonic form).

Similarly, our stage of psychological development reflects in our attitude and approach to religion.  Please remember that we're referring only to the psychological stages here, not to physical, age-group or cultural definitions of these stages – otherwise it can sound insulting to call a grown-up person a child or a group of people children.  To create an analogy for the purposes of this talk, it might be helpful to match up the three levels of education – primary, secondary and tertiary – with these three general stages of psychological development.  Primary, elementary or "grammar" school is likened to our childhood, junior high school to early adolescence, senior high school to late adolescence, and university to maturity.  This is only an analogy and shouldn't be taken too strictly or literally.

Religion suited to childhood is built somewhat around fear.  Gods and spirits are placated and cajoled into doing one's will, or at least to not do any harm.  Blind imitation and mindless acceptance of beliefs is standard.  The rational mind is not yet developed enough for the person to question and think for himself or herself, so the most impossible things are believed.  It's the child's magical Peter Pan world.  This kind of religion is full of appeals to statues and idols of gods or saints, with lots of ceremonies and belief in miraculous events.  Bells, incense, chants and incantations, repeated prayers, making signs, going through rituals, fill such a devotee's life.  Group singing or even dancing make all feel a sense of oneness and upliftment.

The early Christian Churches were just swamped by "pagan" beliefs and practices as well as the popular theology of the time – while the core of Jesus' teachings was pushed into the background.  Despite Jesus' speaking out so vigorously against any priesthood, the old priestly orders flooded in.  Today, the celebration of the resurrection of Jesus occurs on Easter.  But Easter (Oestre) is the name of a pagan goddess of fertility whose symbols are eggs and rabbits.  The words [o]estrus (referring to when an animal is "in heat") and [o]estrogen (a female sex hormone) derive from the name of this goddess.  December 25th is supposed to celebrate the birthday of Jesus (though He was most likely born in the spring).  This was the day that the birthday of Mithra, the founder of then popular Mithraism, was celebrated.  Yet a pagan Santa Claus, winter solstice Yuletide practices with Christmas trees, and remnants of the celebration of the drunken Roman Saturnalia still upstage Jesus on this day.  Hallowe'en (Hallowed Evening) is the eve of the day meant to celebrate all the saints, yet a very old tradition meant to scare away evil spirits completely overwhelms it.  This is just a small testimony to the degree to which Christianity, particularly the older Catholic and Eastern Orthodox Churches, were overwhelmed by entirely non-Christian pagan practices.  Yet, as we see, it all suits the stage of psychological childhood.  (My step-mother's family originates from an island, Ischia, off the coast of Naples in Italy.  She tells of a man screaming with rage at a statue of Mary because she failed to keep her side of a bargain he believed that he had made with her to make him win in a big gambling venture.  This is much in the spirit of religion adapted to childhood.)

Since a person in the stage of psychological childhood has weak powers of reasoning, is unable to distinguish fantasy from reality well, and will believe most anything that an authority says is true, even if utterly irrational, we find millions accepting without question the story of creation and the Garden of Eden as being literally true.  A thinking mind cannot possibly accept this story as being realistic, but to those believing in unreasonable and unrealistic things this is a sign of faith.  Creationism is really about religion for the stage of psychological childhood.

Junior high school represents here the end of childhood and the beginning of adolescence.  The rational mind begins to function in adolescence.  So, religion tends to move from the "feel good" spirit of the child to something more rational, or even intellectual.  Sermons become the focus, the singing and ceremony lose their dominance and dancing disappears.  This is where the intellectualism of theology becomes important.  This is comparable to the belief system and attitude of many in Christian Intelligent Design, where an outwardly scientific and rational approach covers up the remnants of magical beliefs in the core of childhood religion that still remains.  The theologians begin to have doubts about many things previously accepted as a result of their intellectual investigations, but still retain the core beliefs that are the products of blind indoctrinated faith. 

Senior high school represents late adolescence where rebellion is in full flower.  The awakening power of rationality finally pushes the person to see the many flaws and superstitions of religion in its childhood form.  Such a person can no longer believe in what the reasoning mind rejects.  Because the original core of the religion has been hidden away and swamped by an ocean of primitive practices and beliefs suited to childhood, this full adolescent finally rejects the entire religion, even all religion, regarding it as just primitive superstition and myth.  Many such people just turn their back on religion to join the throngs of the irreligious or non-religious.  But some, especially if indoctrinated in the universities, become adamant crusaders for atheism and, being unable to eject a religious attitude, remain turned towards religion, but in a posture of defiance and opposition.  (You have to face someone and interact closely with them to fight them. This is why "We tend to become like those we oppose". This is quite different from just turning one's back on religion. Salman Rushdie quipped, "Atheists are obsessed with God".)  A previous talk spoke of how, as a result of scholars of the major religions translating the works of Aristotle and other Greek philosophers in Islamic Spain, a new movement began that established secular universities motivated by a complete rejection of the Roman Church (which had originally dominated all education).  Rather than simply becoming free and independent of religion and the Church, they become reactionary and adopted a quite fundamentalist and missionary atheism.   Notice how missionary atheists such as Richard Dawkins are continually interacting with the religious in this negative way in their holy war against standard religion.

If creationism is elementary school religion and Christian Intelligent Design is junior high school religion, then atheist materialist Darwinism is the expression of adolescent senior high school religion.  This is the evolution of evolution.  But all the stages are present together.  Rather than view these as right or wrong, one can also see them as necessary stages in the unfolding of our human understanding of our origin and evolution.

In a sense, and even from a religious viewpoint, there's a great value in atheism because, even though it throws out the baby with the bath water, it is at least getting rid of the filthy bath water of superstitions.  Later they can, in maturity, recognize the baby they tossed out and realize that there was no good reason to toss out that baby itself – which they never saw originally, so deeply was it hidden within the murk – and can retrieve it freed from the filth of unscientific superstition that had once utterly covered it.  If atheists deny the existence of a superman personal god in the sky – who never existed anyway other than in imagination – then they do us all a service.  If they toss out the mind-crippling irrational beliefs, the primitive ceremonies and magical nonsense, then we can only benefit.  Sometimes we have to reject something completely in order to be able to get cleansed of the garbage and then come back to look at it objectively and find the original core that was ignored but now freed from its filth.  

The cup has to be emptied of its old dirty water before the fresh clean water can be poured in – otherwise the new clean water becomes polluted with the dirty old water. The ugly crawling caterpillar has to sacrifice itself in the cocoon before the beautiful butterfly can emerge and fly away.  The rebellion of adolescence, however unconstructive it might seem, is a necessary stage that frees a person sufficiently to look for truth unhampered by the immense load of superstitious belief that once burdened a person.

After the passionate rebellion of the senior high school stage comes the calm, rational wisdom of the university of maturity. This is just an analogy and is not suggesting that the very adolescent rebellious spirit of our present-day universities is in any way now mature and wise.  The mature mind finally realizes that rejecting the notion of the superman god in the sky simply tells us that there's no such god in our world that is created in our image.  But it says nothing about whether or not a transcendent God behind all of existence exists.  If the atheists deny existence to what is transcendent, then they clearly adopt this as an article of faith just as much as those who believe in the existence of the transcendent Being – and this would make atheism a genuine "religious" belief system.  Science itself, by the materialists' own definition, can say nothing whatsoever about what transcends the physical world. 

Albert Einstein also argued that there are three stages of religion or religious belief.  He spoke of the first kind as based on fear and the second kind as "moral religion", one that seeks to inculcate good behavior rather than just manipulate the gods or saints and other powers.  This latter is a more intellectual religion.  But he says that the highest form of religion is that in which a mature person is moved by a "cosmic religious feeling".  He writes:

"The Jewish scriptures admirably illustrate the development from the religion of fear to moral religion, a development continued in the New Testament. The religions of all civilized peoples, especially the peoples of the Orient, are primarily moral religions. The development from a religion of fear to moral religion is a great step in peoples' lives. And yet, that primitive religions are based entirely on fear and the religions of civilized peoples purely on morality is a prejudice against which we must be on our guard. The truth is that all religions are a varying blend of both types, with this differentiation: that on the higher levels of social life the religion of morality predominates. 

"Common to all these types is the anthropomorphic character of their conception of God. In general, only individuals of exceptional endowments, and exceptionally high-minded communities, rise to any considerable extent above this level. But there is a third stage of religious experience which belongs to all of them, even though it is rarely found in a pure form: I shall call it cosmic religious feeling.  It is very difficult to elucidate this feeling to anyone who is entirely without it, especially as there is no anthropomorphic conception of God corresponding to it. 

"The individual feels the futility of human desires and aims and the sublimity and marvelous order which reveal themselves both in nature and in the world of thought. Individual existence impresses him as a sort of prison and he wants to experience the universe as a single significant whole. The beginnings of cosmic religious feeling already appear at an early stage of development, e.g., in many of the Psalms of David and in some of the Prophets.  Buddhism, as we have learned especially from the wonderful writings of Schopenhauer, contains a much stronger element of this." (Albert Einstein, New York Times Magazine, November 9, 1930, pp 1-4) [emphasis mine]

Einstein gives us a hint of that stage of religion that moves beyond the high school adolescence of rebellion, that comes to a more balanced, rational and scientific approach where religion and science, though distinct, work in harmony to balance and moderate each other. This background now gives us the foundations for understanding the following comments on Christian Intelligent Design and Darwinist evolutionary theory.  If a person with this mature conception of religion or faith interacts with an atheist, this atheist will usually assume that he or she is coming from the childhood expression of religion and argue correspondingly – all quite unaware that there is a world of difference between the two.

CHRISTIAN INTELLIGENT DESIGN

Certain Christians have taken over the label Intelligent Design (ID) for themselves, perhaps in the hopes of making people believe that theirs is the only version of it and that any who are convinced by its arguments might naturally follow a path of belief into the Christian religion.  This is little different from the Darwinist practice of claiming that "believing in evolution" means acceptance of the particular Darwinian view of how it happens.  Darwinists accuse the Christians of using Intelligent Design – which speaks outwardly only of scientific matters – of being a Trojan horse with the "agenda" of Christian dogma hidden within.  And any who study Darwinism or neo-Darwinism with any care quickly realize that it too is a Trojan horse containing an entire atheist materialist philosophy and program hidden behind it.  And this is why anyone who investigates with care the war raging between the fundamentalist Christians and the fundamentalist Darwinists realizes before long that all the outward talk about science and what should be taught in schools is, at a much deeper level, really a kind of holy war going on between a narrow Christian theism and secular atheism – a war that has been going on since the secularists founded their universities almost a millennium ago on the principle of opposition to the Roman Church and religion in general.  Interestingly, both sides in this war maintain a materialist view; and this is what hinders both from doing good science.

There is a large number of scientists, most of them physical scientists of no particular religious persuasion, who are convinced that the Anthropic Principle (explained in the previous talk) with its evidence of top-down structuring in the universe, a System Theory and ecological view of our world, and perhaps a grasp of some logical arguments, lead inexorably to the conclusion of an intelligently designed universe.  A physical scientist who is a Fellow of the Royal Society wrote a letter to The Times of London that evolutionist Richard Dawkins quotes in his Climbing Mount Improbable (pp 64-65).  Here's a bit of that letter.

"Sir, I am one of the physical scientists … who doubt Darwin's theory of evolution.  My doubts arise not from any religious motive or desire to add fuel to either side of any controversy but merely because I think that Darwinism is scientifically indefensible.

"We have no option but to accept evolution – all the fossil evidence points to it.  The contention is only about the cause….

"… It seems to me impossible that chance variation should have produced the remarkable machine that is the human body.  … I myself can see no alternative to the hypothesis that living matter was designed. …"

There are many other physical scientists and mathematicians who make the same protest from purely scientific, mathematical and logical considerations – without any religious agenda to promote.   This view, and that of many other scientists – one that accepts the fact of evolution but rejects many aspects of the Darwinian view in favor of some kind of intelligent design – we might call intelligently designed evolution.  This distinguishes it from any of (neo)Darwinian evolution, Christian Intelligent Design, and creationism. 

This letter from the British physical scientist contrasts strongly with the bold pronouncement so repeatedly stated by defenders of Darwinian evolutionary theory that virtually all scientists support "evolution" – by which they intend to mean Darwinian or neo-Darwinian evolution.  Such scientists and not a few mathematicians are troubled not by the fact of evolution itself, which they accept, but rather by some of the serious weaknesses of Darwinian explanations for how evolution works.

To repeat, this means that the impression conveyed by political squabblers that one hears or reads in the news that all those believing in intelligent design are of the religious Intelligent Design (ID) people is very misleading.  But it's these latter ones with the religious agenda that get most of the attention.  And for Darwinists these religious ID people are much easier to deal with than are secular scientists who ask very difficult questions that Darwinist views are not now able to handle well.  So, a supposed victory over the ID people is falsely portrayed as a victory over the more general intelligent design.  And this is no better than the unreasonable creationist or ID approach of imagining that showing defects in Darwinian views of how evolution works means that evolutionary change itself doesn't happen.  This is bad reasoning on both sides.  When issues become political and highly-charged with impassioned attachment to particular "sides", then truth, reason and science suffer.   In fact, such a war zone is just the place to look for some of the worst abuses of logic and science.

Some of the Intelligent Design (ID) people with a religious-political agenda wish, like the creationists, to find arguments against the fact of evolution itself.  Others accept that the fact of evolution is a reality, but find problems with Darwinian explanations for the mechanisms behind this evolution.  And these latter may not accept the view that one must choose between a Supreme Designer and the principles at work in Nature; they may see these principles that scientists study in Nature as simply the means by which a design that is created at a higher level is carried out at this lower level.

In sum, the simplistic, fairy-tale, black and white, "good guys" and "bad guys" scenario that both sides might wish to portray in the political-religious war conveys a very wrong picture of the reality here.  It's more complex than this, and the real scientific solution to the matter of biological evolution could very well exist outside the arena in which these fierce defenders of their respective true faiths do their battle.

 

The proponents of Christian Intelligent Design (ID) are positioning themselves in a situation where they are at a disadvantage.  By being educated in the universities in a materialist view of reality they usually accept unquestioningly the materialist and accidentalist view of the world – which we've shown in the previous talks are unscientific superstitions.  When Darwinism falls as a consequence of a gradual awakening to its being based on the shaky foundation of these unsupported assumptions of materialism and accidentalism, Christian ID must inevitably go down with it – unless they free themselves more than they do now. Irreducible Complexity

Biochemist Michael Behe, author of several books advocating Intelligent Design such as Darwin's Black Box and Molecular Machines : Experimental Support for the Design Inference tells us that irreducible complexity means that a system works only when all its parts are there, that if one part is left out it won't work. He argues that there are many such systems in the bodies of living things that are like this such as the whiplike tails (flagella) of tiny one-celled animals that they use to propel themselves, the cascade of events by which blood clots, and the adaptive immune system.

Darwinists respond by arguing that taking a part or more away from some such system may result in a different setup that has other quite different purposes so that the evolution of the system has different functions along the way towards the system as we see it.  They point out how whales, for example, have a blood-clotting mechanism where one of the elements required in us humans is missing.  The ID scientists could repond with: How very remarkable (and stupendously improbable) that a system would have a practical use each step of the way in its evolution! Behe's arguments seem to suggest that gradual evolution won't work – even if we accept the Darwinist's view that each step in the building up of a system has a practical use. RANDOM VARIATION  AND  NATURAL SELECTION

Darwinism is built around the twin ideas of random variation and natural selection.  Living organisms change through random variation, usually mutations.  Then, if they are "bad" mutations that don't help the organism live and adapt well, then it dies out because it doesn't breed.  The few "good" mutations or variations make an organism more adapted and this leads to success in mating that propagates the new trait.  That is, natural selection works like a filter that passes the good variations and lets them multiply while rejecting the bad ones.  Some call this "survival of the fittest".  They often think of it in very militant terms as being the survival of the strongest, but it's often those who can avoid fighting, or who are more "intelligent" than strong, that survive. For us, it was survival of the mentally fittest.  Some even say that perhaps in the future, the principle will be survival of the spiritually fittest – so that the meek will inherit the earth and filter out all the more primitive kinds of people who are stupid enough to make war and trouble for their fellow humans.

One big problem with the idea of random variation is that it's based on the superstition of accidentalism; and that tells us that it won't work.  Randomness is actually hidden order with regular causes.  Evidence for this is seen when viruses and protozoans (single-celled animals) that bring us disease so quickly "mutate" into a form that is resistant to our drugs and that is more effective in attacking us.  The many thousands of years required for good mutations to appear by chance are simply not available. These tiny disease organisms find excellent solutions in a relatively short space of time.  That means that there's some design or guidance at work; it's not just random chance.

Even if we were to accept variation as being "random" in the sense of being disordered and aimless, Darwinists expect far too much of it than probability can produce.  They have an unrealistic idea of the strength of probability.  In fact, it's extremely weak and cannot possibly account for the stunning order and complexity of organization of living things. Chance is far too weak to do the things that Darwinists expect of it.

There are other arguments from physics to show that mere chance is not enough to create effective evolutionary change. The Fossil Record

A study of the record of fossils does not in the least support the idea that there is gradual change in evolution.  Darwin assumed that this was due to the spottiness of the fossil record and that in the future it would fill out and show us this gradual change.  Well, we're in that future now and the fossil record is much more complete now – and there is still no sign in it of gradual change.  It shows stasis – long periods of time where species show very little change – followed by very abrupt change to such a degree that many biologists simply assume that these radically new forms are different species.  The way so many Darwinists remain in denial of this speaks of the degree to which indoctrination and dogma parading as science have taken over.

About every 26 million years on average, an immense calamity seems to strike the earth and wipe out most living things.  This has happened at least 20 times.  After each catastrophe, as if by magic, an entirely new set of forms suddenly appears and diversifies wildly. This is called radiation.  Darwinism is unable to explain it.  It does not accord with gradualism.  If we consider how a butterfly is a transformed caterpillar, this idea might be translated to the larger scale of evolution to suggest to us that species might also change their forms dramatically at times such as these great catastrophes that mark the divisions between the periods and eras of the geological-fossil record.  Darwin himself was stunned and puzzled by the relatively sudden appearance of flowers and the bees that pollinate them 136 million years ago at the end of the calamity that marked the start of the Cretaceous period (which was the last period of the Mesozoic Era of the dinosaurs).

Paleontologists, the people who study fossils, know these things quite well.

"The known fossil record is not, and never has been in accord with gradualism." (The New Evolutionary Timetable: Fossils, Genes, and the Origin of Species (New York: Basic Books, 1981), p. 71)

"It is not even possible to make a caricature of evolution [Darwinian gradualism] out of paleo-biological facts.  The fossil material is now so complete that ... the lack of transitional series cannot be explained by the scarcity of the material.  The deficiencies are real, they will never be filled." (Heribert Nilsson of Lund University, Synthetische Artbildung (Lund, Sweden: Gleerup, 1954), English Summary, p. 1212) (The New Biology, pp 160-61)

Beetles, sturgeon, garpikes, alligators, tapirs, aardvarks, ants, many molluscs and echinoderms have gone unchanged for millions of years.  Bowfin fishes and lungfish have barely changed in hundreds of millions of years.  Turtles aren't much different from what they were 180 million years ago.  Cockroaches are much as they were 280 million years ago.  The horseshoe crab (which is more like an ancient trilobite and definitely not a crab) hasn't changed in 500 million years.  Why wouldn't gradual evolution apply to these?  This lack of change is called stasis.

But the Darwinists struggle mightily to try to rationalize their way out of this so as to maintain Darwin's hypothesis of gradual change – which is in defiance of the scientific method that tells us to change the hypothesis to fit the evidence, not change the evidence to fit the hypothesis. Gould and Eldridge call this  punctuated equilibrium. 

Actually, changes in a part of a body won't work any more than putting wings and a propeller on a bus will make it fly.  Sticking wings on a horse as with Pegasus just won't work. "The application of these three new concepts [dual wave-particle nature, indeterminism, etc.] results in the breakdown of an assumption which lies behind much of our customary language and way of thinking; namely, that the world can correctly be analyzed into distinct parts each having a separate existence, but working together according to exact causal laws to form the whole.
