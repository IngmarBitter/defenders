## Yom

Yes, it does not have only the meaning of 24h.  A *long unspecified time* is **possible**. However, we must ask if it is **reasonable** to believe that in Genesis chapter 1 does intend the meaning of a *long unspecified time*. The common plain sense of the word is a 24h day. I like this rule of thumb: *"When the plain sense makes common sense, use no other sense, lest it be nonsense"*.

* Context determines deviation from common sense (***italics*** if >24h)
  * In my father’s ***day***, he spent **six days** crossing the country. He would go to bed in the **evening** and rise in the **morning** of the following **day**, traveling only during the **day**.
  * $\to$ The first ***day*** here is a *long unspecified time* which is clear form the context. All others are either 24h or shorter.
* All these contexts are in Genesis
  * 1:5 God called the light **day**, and the darkness He called **Night**. So the **evening** and the **morning** were **day one**.
  * 1:8 So the **evening** and the **morning** were the **second day**.
  * 1:14 … let them [sun, moon, stars] be signs to indicate **seasons**, and **days**, and **years**.
  * 2:4 ***When [in-the-day-that]*** the Lord God made the earth and the heavens, 
  * 2:17 ***When [in-the-day-that]*** you eat from it you will surely die.
  * 3:14 You are cursed … you will eat dust **all the days** of your life
  * $\to$ Only ***When [in-the-day-that]***  is a *long unspecified time*, because the context is a Hebrew idiom best translated as ***when***. All others are 24h or shorter as can be seen from the immediate context of the verse.
* Creation week is a model for the human week
  * Exodus 20:11 **For in six days** the Lord made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and **rested on the seventh day**. **Therefore** the Lord blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.
* All 359 uses of **day + number** in the OT outside Genesis mean 24h day (incl. history, prophecy, wisdom, poetry)
* All 53 uses of **day + night** in the OT outside Genesis mean normal day (incl. history, prophecy, poetry)
* **Day + evening + morning** always means a normal day
* None of the church fathers interpreted yom as a *long unspecified time*. 

Combined those lead me to believe that **day + number + morning + evening** can not be reasonably considered to mean >> 24h.  Where is the flaw in my analysis?
