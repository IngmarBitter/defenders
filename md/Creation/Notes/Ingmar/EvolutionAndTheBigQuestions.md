## Introduction

Stamos begins by praising the wealth of evidence for the theory of *evolution*, making it “one of the greatest and most solid of human achievements, possibly even the greatest of all time. \[…\] to deny evolution is to deny the very nature and value of evidence itself.” And to deny *evolution* he assumes must involve evidence free or evidence contrary reasoning which “invites moral condemnation”.

Unfortunately he is not consistent in what he means by *evolution*. In the glossary he defines it as “change in a species \[…\] that has a genetic basis”, with *random DNA change* and *natural selection* being two key concepts. However, later in the introduction and in the appendix on “*Misconceptions About Evolution*” the concept of *common descent* resulting in a hundreds of millions of years old tree of life is added to the meaning of *evolution*, *evolutionary biology* and *patters of evolution*. This extended meaning is what is most commonly used elsewhere, so that is what I will assume going forward unless otherwise stated: *evolution = random DNA change + natural selection + common descent*. Without the third component we typically speak of *adaptation* = *random DNA change + natural selection.*

Stamos next kicks in high gear against “belief unsupported by or opposed to evidence”. We have a “personal and social duty” to avoid it like a “spreading disease”. As example he gives *intelligent design* and *creationism* which he sees as synonyms and unscientific and about which the public has been mislead and “bamboozled by an enormous propaganda machine”. At the end of the introduction he reemphasizes that “’to gain education a man must study in more than one school’” and that “the greatest enemy to just reasoning is the ‘dogmatic frame of mind’”.

Let’s put his own line of argument to his own test criteria: He apparently has not studied in more than one school, as his portrait of *intelligent design* and *creationism* are very incorrect. He dogmatically states that they are “mythological thinking in a lab coat” and in the appendix adds that the debate about evolution within the professional science community ceased a long time ago with evolution becoming accepted as a fact. In essence an even larger propaganda machine than the one supposedly behind intelligent design and creationism. He conveniently omits sharing what he thinks the real definition of *intelligent design* and *creationism* are, but in the appendix we find that he appears to attribute at least fixivity of species to the *creationism* concept.

A definition for *creationism* in the terms used above is *creationism = random DNA change + natural selection + common design*. So we have God creating the initial kinds that are not today’s species, and then we have since the fall in the garden of Eden the principles of *random DNA change* and *natural selection* at work producing the species we find in the fossil record and alive today. That gives us a theory that is a rational alternative to evolution theory, since all direct evidence for *random DNA change* and *natural selection* obviously supports the common part of both theories equally, and the indirect evidence for *common descent* actually can be seen with equally rational justification as evidence for *common design* – i.e. commonality in DNA pattern or in appearance is either due to recent common ancestry or to more reused parts. So if based on the evidence *evolution* is a fact, then so is *creation*ism. Since both cannot be a fact simultaneously we see that the use of the word fact is inappropriate for both – they are competing theories.

A definition for *intelligent design* in the terms used above is *intelligent design* *= random DNA change + natural selection + change quantification*. Here we have no religion whatsoever, only scientific observation and conclusions. In analyzing and quantifying the change from one generation to another and its progress over time it is tested if the processes of *random DNA change + natural selection* alone are sufficient to produce the bio diversity we find today. The result is that there are several examples of biological functionality that are *irreducibly complex* and could in principle never have been produced via those processes alone. The only rationally acceptable explanation for the new information content in the DNA that codes for the *irreducible complex* systems is that an *intelligent designer* produced them, hence the name for this branch of science.

In the appendix Stamos discusses *evolution* further as being “genuine science” which includes falsifiability, for which he gives two examples:\
(1) Evolution would be falsified if “a human skull were to be found in a rock strata formed 100 million years ago”. Contrary to his assertion two very similar finds exist:\

(2) Evolution would be falsified if “each species had its own genetic code”. Actually, even one instance of a different genetic code, or different DNA language would suffice, since there is no way a mutation that causes a change in the meaning of the DNA letter encodings can lead to a living offspring. (a) But all mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) has a different DNA code than nuclear DNA – even though the mtDNA supposedly evolved incrementally from bacteria nuclear DNA. See [link](http://creation.com/mitochondria-created-to-energize-us) and [link](http://creation.com/images/pdfs/tj/j21_3/j21_3_84-92.pdf) . (b) Another counterexample is the DNA of Paramecium in which some triplet codons code for different aminoacids. See sidebar at [link](http://creation.com/dna-marvellous-messages-or-mostly-mess) .\
Together with the *intelligent design* research results on *irreducible complex* systems that never could have evolved these three arguments make a good case for *evolution* actually being falsified and thus clearly inferior to the *intelligent design* and *creationism* theories which both handle these three problem categories well. And there are many more such examples. See [www.creation.com](http://www.creation.com) , [www.answersingenesis.org](http://www.answersingenesis.org) .

A final point of contention with the appendix is the section on *Origin of Life*. Stamos admits that DNA based life is too complicated to get started by chance. He postulates that there was an earlier different type of self replicating life that allowed for mutations and natural selection to be the scaffolding to eventually get to DNA based life. There are various problems here: (a) in the section on “*Missing Links*” he explains that evolution to higher life forms does not displace the lower form, hence we still find bacteria today. By the same argument we should still find those DNA life precursors, but we do not! (b) He states that there are five competing main theories for the origin of life. So clearly none of them is convincing, as otherwise it would have won the competition long ago. He proceeds to explain one of them – presumably the one he finds most plausible. It postulates a self replicating molecule that operates similar to crystals. Unfortunately crystals grow according to repetitive patterns governed by chemical laws. That gives us no possibility of inheritance of random pattern variations, which is essential for evolution to get started. (c) If the first life molecules were not crystal like, but RNA, then we have the problem that RNA is less stable than DNA, does not self assemble in nearly long enough chains, still needs enzymes to copy and still needs error correction in the copy mechanism to avoid error catastrophe – all not possible by chance. (d) Finally the Miller Urey experiment is marketed as a success in origin of life research, while it really was an utter failure: it proved that even with intelligent input and refined ingredients nothing remotely similar to DNA, or proteins or enzymes let alone a simple cell was generated. The best was a subset of the needed most basic building blocks, amino acids. By analogy, you cannot make a fancy multicolored lego statue by shaking a box of uni-colored legos for a few billion years. They newer self assemble in the right shape and newer change color on their own. A devastating review of the experiment is available here: [link](http://creation.com/why-the-miller-urey-research-argues-against-abiogenesis) .

In summary, Stamos reiterates standard evolution propaganda in a dogmatic, closed mind, evidence contradicting manner which is diametrically opposed to the academic and scientific standards he claims to follow.

To conclude the review of Stamos view of *evolution* as a fact, since the remainder of his book rests mostly on the principles of *random variation* and *natural selection,* we can just substitute the word *adaptation* whenever he uses *evolution* resulting in an acceptable foundation for the remainder of the book. Note that *adaptation* is a common component to all three theories, so there is no controversy no matter which theory someone might prefer.

Next Stamos attacks the *Standard Social Science Model* (SSSM) which claims that human behavior is determined exclusively by “*nurture*” (culture and conditioning) and denies human “*nature*” (any genetic influence). With the SSSM dominating social sciences and humanities Stamos wants to explore how far evolutionary explanations can reach to displace common SSSM based explanations. He clarifies that the key contention is not *nature* vs. *nurture*, but *nature-nurture* vs. *nurture*, where the *nature* in *nature-nurture* influences behavior but does not determine it.

SSSM leads to “cultural relativism” in which there is no right and wrong culture and morality is merely a “convenient term for socially approved habits”.

SSSM can also not explain how language acquisition functions, a universal grammar understanding genetically hand-wired into our brains is needed which is part of sociobiology studies.

SSSM is further problematic in over emphasizing political correctness to the point of censorship in areas such as racism.

In other cases the SSSM is valid, so Stamos wants to explore which parts of it can remain and which should evolve, i.e. where evolutionary concepts should be integrated or used instead - and this in the context of knowledge acquisition, consciousness, language, mating, rape, homosexuality, incest, feminism, racism, ethics, religion, and the meaning of life.
