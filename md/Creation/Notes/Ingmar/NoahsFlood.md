# God saved Noah from the flood 

>  Gen 6:5 - 9:17


>   5  The Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great on the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was continually only evil.   6  The Lord was sorry that He had made man on the earth, and it grieved Him in His heart.   7  So the Lord said, “I will destroy man, whom I have created, from the face of the earth—both man and beast, and the creeping things, and the birds of the sky, for I am sorry that I have made them.”   

God is seeing evil character in most people. He grieved. He is sad over the outcome of letting people make their own decisions.

>   8  But Noah found grace in the eyes of the Lord.

Noah is the exception. God decided to spare Noah. To extend grace to him.

>   9  These are the generations of Noah.

These are the generations of ..., a typical phrase when starting the historical account of the next patriach in the geneology of Christ. => not poetry, but historical account.

> Noah was a just man and blameless among his contemporaries. Noah walked with God.   10  Noah had three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

Noah was respected and of good character as seen by men and as seen by God.

>   11  The earth was corrupt before God and filled with violence.   12  God looked on the earth and saw it was corrupt, for all flesh had corrupted their way on the earth. 

This verse talks about ALL flesh, which is universal, but here it is right after the prior verse exhonorating Noah. So from the context it's clear that Noah is excluded from all the others that are corrupted. This exclusion is also implied in the next few verses.

>  13  So God said to Noah, “The end of all flesh is come before Me, for the earth is filled with violence because of them. Now I will destroy them with the earth.   14  Make an ark of cypress wood for yourself. Make rooms in the ark, and cover it inside and out with pitch.   15  And this is how you must make it: The length of the ark will be 137m, the width of it 23m, and the height of it 14m.   

2 1/4 the width of our house and 3x the length of our house and 5 instead of two stories. In total our house would fit 17 times into the ark.

>16  Make an opening one cubit below the top of the ark all around; and you must set the door of the ark on the side. Make it with a lower, a second, and a third story.   

quite detailed building instructions. This does not read like poetry. We ought to believe that this is an actual account of what God commanded Noah.

>  17  I will bring a flood of waters on the earth to destroy all flesh, wherever there is the breath of life under heaven, and everything that is on the earth will die.   18  But I will establish My covenant with you; you must go into the ark—you, and your sons, and your wife, and your sons’ wives with you.   19  Bring every living thing of all flesh, two of every kind, into the ark to keep them alive with you. They shall be male and female.   20  Two of every kind of bird, of every kind of animal, and of every kind of creeping thing of the earth will come to you to be kept alive.   21  Also, take with you of every kind of food that is eaten and gather it to yourself, and it will be for food for you and for them.”

Those are clear descriptions of what God is planning to do. The flood will destroy all flesh, humans and animals alike. This can only mean global across the earth. Because otherwise, what would be the point of sending two of each kind to the ark? The only interpretation of this passage that makes sense to me is that the purpose of sending the animals was to save them from extinction. And that in turn only makes sense if God was planning a global flood covering the whole globe.

>   22  Noah did this; he did all that God commanded him.

Noah was a good servant of God. He must have endured a lot of ridicule from people around him for building such a large boat on dry ground. But he was more concerned what God thought about his actions than what people thought.

>   1  The Lord said to Noah, “You and your entire household go into the ark, for you alone I have seen to be righteous before Me among this generation.   2  Take with you seven each of every clean animal, the male and its female, and two each of every unclean animal, the male and its female,   3  and seven each of birds of the air, the male and female, to keep offspring alive on the face of all the earth.   4  In seven days I will cause it to rain on the earth for forty days and forty nights, and every living thing that I have made I will destroy from the face of the earth.”
>   5  And Noah did according to all that the Lord commanded him.

Here we get a little more details of God's plan and timeline. The command to take seven of the clean animals is so that Noah has some animals that he can sacrifice when the flood is over and to have a quicker restart of his herds.

>   6  Noah was six hundred years old when the floodwaters came upon the earth.   7  And Noah went with his sons and his wife and his sons’ wives into the ark because of the floodwaters.   8  Everything that creeps on the land from clean and unclean animals and birds   9  came in two by two, male and female, to Noah into the ark, as God had commanded Noah.   10  After seven days, the waters of the flood were on the earth.

So now we read about the execution of the flood. Everything happens just as God said it would. First in this summary verse and then in more detail in the next few verses.

>   11  In the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, in the **second month, on the seventeenth day of the month**, the same day, all the fountains of the great deep burst open and the floodgates of the heavens were opened.   12  The rain fell upon the earth for forty days and forty nights.
>   13  On the very same day Noah and the sons of Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and Noah’s wife, and the three wives of his sons with them, entered the ark.   14  They and every wild animal according to its kind, and all the livestock according to their kind, and every creeping thing that creeps on the earth according to its kind, and every bird according to its kind, every bird of every sort,   15  went with Noah into the ark, two by two of all flesh in which was the breath of life.   16  So they went in, male and female of all flesh, just as God had commanded him; then the Lord shut him in.

Once all people and animals were in the ark, God closed the ark. There were no windows on the sides. So Noah could not see anyone outside. And since the rain was severe, we should think of it more as a hurricane. No Noah would have heard the noise of the storm and not be able to hear any person possibly crying for help.  Some of the recent Hollywood movies about Noah's flood paint very viciously misleading pictures of this. They have Noah on the top deck of the ark frantically fighting off the poor other people that in droves try to get on the ship to be saved but only to be defeated by Noah. Nothing could be further from the truth. Noah was shut in and could not see or reach out or communicate with anyone outside. There was no lack of compassion on the side of Noah. But God wanted to punish the continuously evil people and was nice enough to shut Noah in so Noah did not have to see how the just punishment outside proceeded.

>   17  The flood was on the earth forty days, and the water increased and lifted up the ark, so that it rose up above the earth.   18  The water prevailed and increased greatly upon the earth, and the ark floated on the surface of the water.   19  The water prevailed exceedingly on the earth, and **all** the high mountains that were under the **whole** heaven were covered.   20  The waters prevailed upward and the mountains were covered 7m deep.   21  **All** flesh that moved on the earth died: birds and livestock and beasts, and **every** creeping thing that crept on the earth, and **every** man.   22  **All** in whose nostrils was the breath of life, all that was on the dry land, died.   23  So He blotted out **every** living thing which was on the face of the ground, both man and animals and the creeping things and the birds of the heavens. They were blotted out from the earth, and only Noah and those who were with him in the ark remained alive.

This passage uses all and every and whole 7 times directly and a few times indirectly. Reading this it's hard to understand how some people interpret this as describing a local flood in the Mesopotamian valley.  Especially the covering of the mountains does not fit with the idea of a local flood. 

* more than 150 flood traditions
* genetic evidence of a human DNA bottleneck down to three women about 3500 years ago. That matches Noah's three son's wives.
* marine fossils found across mountains all over the world. Lots of sea shells and fish, but even wales.

>   24  The waters prevailed on the earth for one hundred and fifty days.

So 1/2 year of the earth being covered in water.

> 1  God remembered Noah and every living thing and all the livestock that were with him in the ark. So God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the water receded.   
After 1/2 the waters started to recede, but with so much water it takes a while.

> > 2  Also the fountains of the deep and the floodgates of heaven were closed, and the rain from the heavens was restrained.   3  The water receded steadily from the earth, and after the end of one hundred and fifty days the waters decreased.   

So it took another 1/2 year for the flood waters to receede.

> 4  The ark rested in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on the mountains of Ararat.   5  The water continually decreased until the tenth month. In the tenth month, on the first day of the month, the tops of the mountains became visible.
>   6  Then at the end of forty days, Noah opened the window of the ark which he had made,   7  and he sent forth a raven, which went to and fro until the waters were dried up on the earth.   8  Then he sent out a dove to see if the waters had subsided from the face of the ground.   9  But the dove found no rest for the sole of her foot, so she returned to him into the ark, for the waters were on the surface of all the earth. Then he put forth his hand, and took her, and brought her into the ark to himself.   10  He waited yet another seven days, and again he sent out the dove from the ark.   11  The dove came to him in the evening, and in her mouth there was a freshly plucked olive leaf. So Noah knew that the waters had receded from the earth.   12  He waited another seven days and sent out the dove again, but it did not return to him again.
>   13  So in the six hundred and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from the earth; and Noah removed the covering of the ark, and looked, and saw the surface of the ground was dry.   14  In the **second month, on the twenty-seventh day** of the month, the earth was dry.

So water was gone after 1 year and 10 days.

>   15  Then God spoke to Noah, saying,   16  “Go out of the ark, you and your wife, and your sons and your sons’ wives with you.   17  Bring out with you every living thing of all flesh that is with you, birds and animals, and every creeping thing that creeps on the earth, so that they may breed abundantly on the earth and be fruitful and multiply on the earth.”
>   18  So Noah and his sons and his wife and his sons’ wives went out.   19  Every beast, every creeping thing, every bird, and everything that moves on the earth, according to their families, went out of the ark.

Noah waited in the protection of the ark till God told him that it is safe to exit. Noah trusted in God's leading.

>   20  Then Noah built an altar to the Lord and took of every clean animal and of every clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar.   21  The Lord smelled a soothing aroma; 

Noah did not have many clean animals, but he decided as his first action to sacrifice one of each as an offernig to the Lord. He wanted to express his gratitude for God saving him from death through the flood.

>   and the Lord said in His heart, “I will never again curse the ground because of man, for the inclination of man’s heart is evil from his youth, nor will I again destroy every living thing as I have done.

Noah's offering is being appreciated by God. And God includes our attitudes and actions toward Him in his plan for the world. Yes, before creating the world God decreed everything that will ever happen, but when doing so He took into consideration what we would do and how we would relate to Him. So this verse covers two points in time, one before time really existed, when He made his plan for the world and also the actual moment in time where God smelled the soothing aroma of the sacrifice and was reminded of his plan that in response He would commit to never again cause a global flood.

* The now know mountain ranges formed during the second half of the flood, when the waters receded.

>           The Noahic Covenant
> 1  Then God blessed Noah and his sons and said to them, “Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth.   2  Every beast of the earth and every bird of the sky and all that moves on the earth and all the fish of the sea will fear you and be terrified of you. They are given into your hand.   3  Every moving thing that lives will be food for you. I give you everything, just as I gave you the green plant.

Here God lifts the vegetarian diet restrictons and allows any type of meat. So next time you have a steak, remember that it is approved by God.

>   4  “Only you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood.   5  But for your own lifeblood I will surely require a reckoning; from every animal will I require it; of man, too, will I require a reckoning for human life, of every man for that of his fellow man.
>           6  Whoever sheds the blood of man,
> by man shall his blood be shed;
> for God made man
> in His own image.

Here God repeats that man shall not kill another man. And there are no age limitations. So wether the other man is a several month before birth or a few month before cancer will destroy his body, God does not want them to be killed. It is only for God to give or take life. Now if this command is violated, then God commands that people shall put the offender to death. When doing so this is not a violation of the command, but executing God ordained punishment. 

>    7  And as for you, be fruitful and multiply; increase abundantly in the earth and multiply in it.”

This is a repeat of the command given to Adam and Eve in the garden of Eden. God is consistent.

>   8  Again God spoke to Noah and to his sons with him, saying,   9  “As for Me, I establish My covenant with you, and with your descendants after you;   10  and with every living creature that is with you, the birds, the livestock, and every beast of the earth with you; of all that comes out of the ark, every beast of the earth.   11  I establish My covenant with you. Never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood. Never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth.”

This covenant is with Noah and his descendants, so for every human on earth today. And this covenant is something God chose to bind himself to. We do not have to do anything for it to remain valid. God committed to sticking to this regardless of how much future wickedness there might be.

>    12  Then God said, “This is the sign of the covenant which I am making between Me and you and every living creature that is with you, for all future generations.   13  I have set My rainbow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of a covenant between Me and the earth.   14  When I bring a cloud over the earth, the rainbow will be seen in the cloud;   15  then I will remember My covenant, which is between Me and you and every living creature of all flesh, and the waters will never again become a flood to destroy all flesh.   16  The rainbow will appear in the cloud, and I will see it and remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth.”
>   17  So God said to Noah, “This is the sign of the covenant that I have established between Me and all flesh that is on the earth.”

In modern society the rainbow has been coopted to represent other ideas, but we should never forget that the original God intended and eternally lasting meaning is that it is a symbol from God for the covenant that he made for Noah and us to never again wipe us of the earth with a flood.

God allowed humans to make their own decisions. When they make many bad ones, heaping sin after sin, it became more and more justified for God to judge and punish them. The whole earth was being judged. But God showed mercy to a few people that had been trusting God instead of following the bad crowd. God gave them grace and saved them.

While this happened some 3500 years ago, we have nearly the same today. Lots of people are living in rejection of God and are violating his commands. They are all heaping sin after sin, making it more and more justified for God to judge and punish them. But God made us for the purpose of having a relationship with Him, to trust and follow him. So just as then he is willing to show mercy and grace. The way He does it now is that He calls us to get to know, love and trust Jesus. And all that do God will spare in the judgement to come, because each of us that follow Jesus is seen by God as someone for whom the justified punishment has already been paid. It was paid by Jesus when he died on the cross for you and me. That was the ultimate display of his love for us. One that we should forever cherish.

We do not know when God will stop watching how we do and respond to his call and will bring judgement. But while there is still time, if you have not entrusted your life to Jesus, then seriously consider why?  God went well beyond the what could be commonly expected to reach out to you. There is lots of evidence that He exists, that he communicated through the Bible, that he took on human form in Jesus and that Jesus died and rose again on the third day. And all of that to help us to choose to accept his gift of eternal life with him through Jesus. So choose to trust Jesus.
