“When Darwin published his theory in 1859 in a book called ‘On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection, it unleashed a firestorm of protest through Europe. At that time, the leading explanation for the diversity of organisms was a theory called special creation. This theory held that all species were created independently, by God, perhaps as recently as 6000 years ago. The theory of special creation also maintained that species were immutable, or incapable of change, and thus had been unchanged since the moment of their creation. Darwin’s ideas were radically different. He proposed that life on Earth was ancient and that species change through time.” Pg. 481

“Typological thinking is based on the idea that species are unchanging types and that variations within species are unchanging types and that variations within species are unimportant or even misleading. Typological thinking occurs in the Bible’s book of Genesis, where God created one of each type of organism ….The idea that types never change eventually began to break down, however….” Pg 482

“In On the Origin of Species, Darwin repeatedly used the phrase descent with modification to describe evolution. By this he meant that the species existing today have descended from other, preexisting species and that species are modified, or change, through time. This view was a radical departure from the pattern of independently created and immutable species embodied in Plato’s work and in the theory of special creation. In essence, the pattern component of the theory of evolution by natural selection makes two claims about the nature of species: (1) They change through time, and (2) they are related by common ancestry.

What is History?

It is a unique event in the past. It is an event that cannot be repeated. Something like it may happen again, but that event cannot happen again. Example is my birth as a unique event. Once an event happens, nothing in the present can affect what happened in the past.

Therefore I cannot use “inductive” repeatable experiments to verify the events of the past.  I cannot drag an event in history and subject it to repeatable experiments.  And this is even truer when we talk about origins, when in their very definitions that are unique non-repeatable events. If fact, we have not been able to recreate even a simulation of any of them.

 

An experiment in the present does not prove what happened in the past. 

It can only tell what may have happened.  It is not up to the Scientist to prove that, that is what actually happened.

Example:  Just because I study many “child births in the present day.” does not I can determine the sequence of events of my actual birth.
