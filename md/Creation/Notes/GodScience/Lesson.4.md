## Slide 13

- A Naturalistic Answer
- For rational people, however, free will is also a problem...
- That is, if we are truly mechanistic beings, down to our cognitive and neural processes, then aren't all our thoughts determined in advance? Are we actually making decisions, or do decisions happen to us in a pre-determined way? …
- The answer is no [even though I am a machine, I still have free-will. It is not an illusion.]
- All mechanistic phenomena have reasons and causes, and the brain is not immune from this logic.
- This is spelled out in a nice book by Daniel Wegner "The illusion of conscious will".
- The neurobiology … is clear that our sensation of conscious choice follows by a substantial lag the actual mechanism of choice made elsewhere in our brains.
- Thus our brains make choices, based on experience, on reason, on happenstance... whatever, and then our consciousness is informed of the result, at which point an idea "pops" into our heads.
- Physics and Chemistry do not have the attributes of choice or reason, only intelligent agents do. So you cannot ascribe those attributes to them.
- He has signed up to a naturalistic contract, but he hasn’t read the fine print.
