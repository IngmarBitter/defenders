## Slide 14

- Mutations
- Mutations are changes in the DNA sequence of a cell's genome
- To affect evolution they need to be passed on to the next generation
- Caused by radiation, viruses, transposons  and mutagenic chemicals, as well as errors that occur during meiosis or DNA replication
- Is there evidence that mutations have created the required structural additions in the required time frame?

## Slide 15

- Characteristics of Mutations
- Random with respect to fitness
- Most decrease fitness
- Body has ways to control mutations
- On rare occasions can be beneficial
- Do they occur enough to be significant?
  - Short answer is: No
- In spite of this, they believe that it plays a role in evolution
- There are theoretical models on how this is possible

## Slide 16

- Lenski Experiment
- Richard Lenski grew e-coli bacteria
- Grew 44,000 generations over 6 years
- Wanted to see if mutations could be the “new” law of variation
- Result:
  - Mutations conferred a fitness benefit
  - Acquired ability to metabolize citrate

## Slide 17

- Significance of Lenski Experiment
- Mutations can confer some survival advantage
- Is this an example of new information? unlikely
- Did he observe a novel structure? No
- Started with bacteria, ended with bacteria
- Started with e-coli, ended with e-coli
- 30,000 * 20 years = 600,000 human years
- E-coli are asexual; humans are sexual

## Slide 24

- It is a reduced or incompletely developed structure that has no function or reduced function, but is clearly similar to function organs or structures in closely related species.
