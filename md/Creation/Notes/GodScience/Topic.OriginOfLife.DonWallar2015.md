## Slide 31

- Substances with the same molecular and structural formulae (e.g. C5O5H10), but which cannot be superimposed one on the other are said to be “chiral” and are called stereoisomers.
- When a polarized light source is passed through a solution of these molecules the plane of polarization is rotated.  They are said to be “optically active”.
- *[image]*
- Homochirality
- 7 Challenges in OOL Research

## Slide 32

- The carbon atom in these molecules is chiral.
- The chemical reaction which produces such molecules will ALWAYS contain equal concentrations of the “left-hand” and “right-hand” stereoisomers.  Such a solution is known as a “racemic” mixture.
- *[image]*
- Homochirality

## Slide 56

- The Genetic Code maps 64 three-letter “codons” to the 20 amino acids (and a “stop” signal) used to construct proteins in living organisms.
- Mutations occur when DNA is “misread”
- Recent research is revealing that the Genetic Code is remarkably optimized to reduce these errors
- Indeed, the Genetic Code has been shown to possess better error-minimization capacity than any other conceivable set of rules that could have been used to construct the Code.
- The Genetic Code
- 7 Challenges in OOL Research

  - These codes direct the binding of histone proteins and transcription factors to DNA and direct such processes as mRNA folding and splicing.
