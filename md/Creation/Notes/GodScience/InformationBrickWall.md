## Slide 11

- DNA Natural Selection
- …
- Mutations are DNA copy mistakes
- Natural Selection prevents animals with bad copy mistakes to pass them to the next generation
  - Assuming the mistake causes bad enough birth defects that the baby will die before growing up
- Insignificant and neutral copy mistakes are not affected by natural selection (babies grow up anyway)
  - If this had been going on for millions of years genetic meltdown would have occurred by now
- Good mutations have been observed
  - They all broke some function, which was nonetheless beneficial
  - Thus no examples of information increase in the DNA
- Natural Selection’s main impact is filtering out bad mutations

## Slide 13

- The Edge of Evolution
- The Edge of Evolution (by Michael Behe, 2007)
  - Malaria is a well studied disease
    - Many vaccinations have been developed
    - They typically be come ineffective after a while
    - DNA analysis shows that in all cases the virus mutated partially self destructive: a pathway to take in or digest nutrition was impaired or broken, resulting in also impairing the intake of the vaccines poison
    - Fast vaccine adaptation was achieved with 1 mutation
    - Very rarely 2 mutations were needed
    - 3 mutations might have happened once in a billion years
    - => 3 mutations is the max of what is possible,      the edge of evolution

## Slide 18

- Irreducible Complexity
- Complicated, but can be copied exactly
- Cannot easily be reduced to simpler systems
  - Without total functionality loss
  - At functionality change not in small steps
  - Remember, for humans only 3 base pairs change in one generation
  - Introducing a new protein takes 1000 base pairs
  - Adapting an existing protein takes 100 base pairs for the new structure and a lot of extra ones to coordinate proper manufacturing time, place and amount as well as transport to the right place: p < 10e-700
