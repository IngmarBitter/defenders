# The Diamond

---

## A Familiar Precedent

![](pics/diamond.png style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Christ's death (Christ Decks 3–6) is a **diamond with many facets**:
  * **Penal Substitution** at the center – the penalty truly borne
  * **Satisfaction**, **Ransom**, **Moral Influence** – each truly so
* Several true motifs held together, **none canceling another**
* A jeweler trims each rough facet until it fits the symmetric whole – so each view here is **cut to the shape that fits the others**
* And the finished stone outshines the sum of its facets – the whole is **more** than the parts

---

## Genesis 1–11: One Diamond

![](pics/diamond.png style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* The same might hold for interpreting Genesis – many views can be true, at least in part, at the same time – each adds a facet:
  * **Historical** – six real 24-hour days, a real Adam: **the core**
  * **Literary** – careful structure: forming, then filling
  * **Purposive** – creation ordered for function, a home for man
  * **Polemic** (a pointed attack on a rival view) – a sharp answer to the pagan myths
  * **Moral** – the Sabbath pattern for our work week
* Our thesis: these are true and **synergetic** facets – together more than their sum; the rest of the deck shows why

---

## The Ten Views: Day = 24 Hours

![](pics/Sunset.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **Young-Earth Creation (YEC)**: six 24-hour days about 6,000 years ago, told accurately – whatever science the text touches, it gets right
* **Literal Naive**: six 24-hour days, told in the obsolete science of Moses' day
* **Verse 1–2 Gap**: ages and a ruined world between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2, then six 24-hour days
* **Day-Gap**: six 24-hour days of divine action, separated by long ages
* **Revelation-Day**: six 24-hour days in which God revealed what He had made over ages

---

## The Ten Views: Day ≠ 24 Hours

![](pics/LongAgesClock.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **Day-Age** or **Progressive Creation**: each day a long age, in the order of the secular scientific timeline
* **Literary Framework**: the days are a literary pattern – forming, then filling – not a chronology
* **Functional Creation**: seven days inaugurate the cosmos as God's temple – no material origins
* **Creation Myth**: Genesis recasts the Egyptian myths to correct their theology
* **Mytho-History**: real history in the figurative dress of myth – the days and trees figurative

---

## Map of the Views

![](pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg style="float: right; width: 15rem")

* The atonement is the precedent: several true facets, none canceling another, each cut to fit the whole

# The Text, Once

---

## Day One: Evening and Morning

![](pics/Sunset.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"**God called the light Day**, and the darkness He called Night. **So the evening and the morning were the first day**."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 1:5 (NKJV)</em></p>

* **Yom** (day) defined on its first use – one word for both: the light period ("God called the light Day"), and light plus dark together ("the first day")
* Each day: a **number** plus **evening and morning** – repeated six times
* Light and day exist **before the sun** (day 4)
* Evening and morning before the sun: a **directional** light, and an Earth already **rotating** from day one – not set spinning on day 4

---

## Day Four: The Lights

![](pics/Cosmos.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"Then God said, 'Let there be lights in the firmament of the heavens [...]; and let them be **for signs** and seasons, and **for days and years**; and let them be for lights [...] **to give light** on the earth'; and it was so. **Then God made two great lights**: the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night. [...] So the evening and the morning were the fourth day."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 1:14–16, 19 (NKJV)</em></p>

* Command, making, and result – all **within day 4**; "days and years" – here yom is clearly the ordinary day
* "For signs": the lights **mark and rule** the days – they do not make them; the day has existed since day 1, as a clock does not make time
* The lesser light also "gives light" – bright, not necessarily self-luminous

---

## Very Good – Then Rest

![](pics/GodSittingOnEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"Then God saw everything that He had made, and indeed **it was very good**. So the evening and the morning were the sixth day."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: 0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 1:31 (NKJV)</em></p>

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and **He rested on the seventh day** from all His work which He had done."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 2:2 (NKJV)</em></p>

* "Very good" – the whole finished creation, before the fall
* Day 7 has no "evening and morning"
* Day 7 has no creative work
  * God's work of creating is finished – God is resting
  * He rests from creating, not from upholding: "My Father has been working until now" (John 5:17)

## The Sabbath Grounded in Creation

![](pics/10CommandmentTablets.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"Six days you shall labor and do all your work, but the seventh day is the Sabbath of the LORD your God. [...] **For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth**, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Exodus 20:9–11 (NKJV)</em></p>

* The same word, the same number: **six days** of work, **one day** of rest
* Repeated as a perpetual sign: "for in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth" (Exodus 31:17)
* No known parallel in the ancient Near East: a weekly rest for all, servants and animals included

## In the Day

![](pics/OpenBible.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, **in the day** that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: 0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 2:4 (NKJV)</em></p>

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"[...] For the LORD God had not caused it to rain on the earth, and there was no man to till the ground; but **a mist went up from the earth** and watered the whole face of the ground."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 2:5–6 (NKJV)</em></p>

* "In the day" (be-yom) – a Hebrew idiom for **"when"**: yom used broadly, with no number and no evening-morning
* Genesis 2 opens its close-up of day 6: no rain yet, no man yet, and the ground watered by a **mist** – the text itself says how (used later against "a rainless earth is fantastic")

![](pics/AdamEve.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"But **from the beginning of the creation**, God 'made them male and female.'"</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Mark 10:6 (NKJV)</em></p>

## The Text – In One Line Each

![](pics/OpenBible.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **Genesis 1**: yom defined on day 1, numbered with evening and morning; day 4 **makes** the lights that mark the days; "very good", then rest

# How to Read Genesis

---

## Exegesis, Eisegesis, Concordism

![](pics/LightOnWords.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **Exegesis** – finding what the text means on its own
* **Eisegesis** – adding one's own ideas into the text
* **Concordism** – reading modern science back into the text
* The question is always: how would the **author and his first audience** have understood it?
* Rule of thumb: "When the **plain sense** makes **common sense**, seek no **other sense**, lest it be **nonsense**"

---

## Genre: History with Artful Structure

![](pics/ScrollWithGoldLetters.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Genesis has historical account, careful wording, high structure, and symbolic meaning – **all in chapter 1**
* **Historical:** Adam and Eve were real people, with a lineage all the way to Jesus; God really made all things in the creation week
* **Symbolic:** Adam = "man", Eve = "mother of all living", God breathing into Adam, God walking with Adam
* Genesis 1 uses the Hebrew narrative verb form (waw-consecutive) of history, not the parallelism of Hebrew poetry (CMI)

---

## Diamond Cutting: What Gets Cut

![](pics/FacetCutting.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **(T1) Exegesis:** a view's **true facet** is what the text itself backs, read as the author and his first audience read it
* Eisegesis and concordism are cut: the rough surface imposed from outside
* **(T2) Compatibility:** a facet must stand **alongside the historical timeline** the genre establishes (slide 26) – a view that must replace it cuts into the core
* So every view is a rough stone: we find its facet and polish off the rest
* Typical rough surface: "structure *instead of* sequence", "function *instead of* matter", "myth *instead of* history", "ages *instead of* days"
* **(T3) Referent:** a figurative reading is **kept** if the history stays (next slides)

---

## Every Figure Has a Referent

![](pics/BookThink.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* A figurative meaning **rides on something concrete** – so a figurative reading can be **kept**: it adds meaning without removing the thing it refers to
* All the senses of Scripture are "founded on the literal" (Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.1, a.10)
* Every sign (*signum*) points to a thing (*res*) (Augustine)
* Two kinds of figure in Genesis 1–3 – and neither empties the history

---

## Figurative Words, Real Events

![](pics/GodWalkingGarden.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* The **words** are figurative, the **event** is real:
* "The LORD God walking in the garden" (Genesis 3:8) → real daily closeness and conversation between God and Adam, in a real place
* "Formed from the dust" (Genesis 2:7) → a body really made of the earth – "to dust you shall return" (Genesis 3:19)
* An anthropomorphism is a figure **of something** – not a figure **of nothing**

---

## Real Things with Figurative Meaning

![](pics/EveReachingForFruit.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Typology: the **thing itself** is the referent, and it also means more:
  * **The two trees** – a moral law carried by a concrete prohibition
  * **The rib** – "bone of my bones" (Genesis 2:23): one flesh in marriage
  * **The serpent** – the real deceiver behind it (Revelation 12:9)
  * **The cherubim** – real guardians of God's holiness
  * **The six days** – Exodus 20:11 needs a real week behind the pattern

---

## Even the Rock Is Real

![](pics/ImmutableRock.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"**The LORD is my rock** and my fortress and my deliverer; My God, my strength, in whom I will trust; My shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Psalm 18:2 (NKJV)</em></p>

* A pure metaphor – yet it needs a **real rock**: big, heavy, not moved by wind, rain, or an angry man, a foundation to build on (Matthew 7:24–25)
* That solidity and reliability God **truly has** – no exceptions

---

## Where Is the Vehicle?

![](pics/TreeDryLush.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* What differs is **where the vehicle is**:
  * In a psalm the rock is out in the world – **no event with a rock** is narrated
  * In Genesis 2–3 the trees, the serpent, and the days are placed **inside the story**
* When the vehicle is an object or event **of the narrative**, the narrative claims it existed
* So "figurative" can add meaning to the history – it cannot subtract the history

---

## How to Read – In One Line Each

![](pics/BookThink.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* **Exegesis** reads out of the text; **eisegesis** and **concordism** read into it
* **T1**: does it come from the text as its first readers heard it? **T2**: can it stand beside the historical timeline?
* **T3**: **every figure has a referent** – figurative words, real events; real things, figurative meaning
* Even "the LORD is my rock" needs a real rock – and when the vehicle sits **inside the narrative**, the narrative claims it

# Cutting the Facets

---

## Diamond Cutting: How Each View Is Cut

![](pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg style="float: right; width: 15rem")

* Ten views, one after another – for each:
  * **The view** – stated fairly, its strengths, the true facet it adds
  * **Polishing** (most kept) or **Cutting** (most goes) – its weaknesses, tested with T1, T2, T3, and the texts we read
* Core first, closest rival last – the chart sorts by science, age, and reading, not by this order
* Each view corrects an error in science or theology (slides 3–4) – we keep what is right and build the best of all

---

## Young-Earth Creation (YEC): The Word *Yom*

![](pics/SixDaysCreation.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* God created everything in **six consecutive 24-hour days**, then rested one day
* Moses recorded **God's historically accurate account**, as God revealed it to him
* **Yom with a number**: 359 times in the Old Testament outside Genesis 1 – always an ordinary day
* **Yom with evening and morning**: always an ordinary day
* Exodus 20:11: the same six days ground our work week – a pattern only ordinary days can set; ages cannot be imitated
* Facet kept: **the historical timeline – the core**

---

## YEC: The Church's Reading

![](pics/ChurchOrdinaryDays.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Hebrew had other words for long ages (for example **olam**) – the author did not use them
* The church's standard reading was **six ordinary days** – most fathers (Basil: "twenty-four hours fill up the space of one day"), Luther, Calvin
* The rare deviation went the other way: Origen and Augustine, allegorizing under Greek influence, made creation **instant** – and Augustine still held the earth under 6,000 years
* No long-age reading of the days arises from the text before the rise of old-earth geology (around 1800)
* Two meanings at once: the **historical account** and the **pattern for our week**

---

## YEC: Objections – The Word *Yom*

![](pics/SixOrdinaryDays.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* "A perfectly feasible interpretation" – but not the only one (Craig, fairly, Excursus 3). His reasons, all about the word yom:
  * **Even if yom means 24 hours here, the wider context may use the days as a metaphor for something longer** → gladly, as a layer **on top of** the literal history – never in its place (the diamond)
  * **"In the day" (Genesis 2:4) uses yom broadly** → yes, as the idiom "when" – no number, no evening-morning
  * **Hosea 6:2 "third day" is non-literal** → the "two … three" idiom (Job 5:19; Amos 1:3): the numbers are not counted, but "quick and sure" only works with short days (Sarfati)

---

## YEC: Objections – Metaphor and Day 7

![](pics/FinishedWeekRest.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **"The arm of the LORD" is a metaphor** → yes, but a metaphor needs a referent – and the days sit inside the narrative, so the narrative claims them (Where Is the Vehicle?)
* **Day 7 has no evening – God is still resting, so day 7 is still running; then it is no 24-hour day, and neither need the others be** → God dedicated **one week** to creating, with one day of rest in it; He is not still resting – "My Father has been working until now" (John 5:17)
  * He then walked with Adam and Eve, and has been at work with His people ever since: not creating any more is a continuing **state**, not a continuing **day**

---

## YEC: Objections – Too Much for Days 1–3

![](pics/LightBeforeSun.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **No sun – days 1–3 cannot be ordinary days** → God made light on day 1; the text has day and night before the sun
  * A directional light on an Earth already turning gives ordinary days from day 1 (slide 12)
* **Trees cannot grow to full size in a day** → no creation day ran on natural law; accelerated growth fits that pattern – God's miracles never make anyone wait (Sarfati); command and result both fall in day 3
* **Water cannot drain off land in a day – the Flood took 150 days (Genesis 8:3)** → that was runoff; day 3 was a command: "and it was so"

---

## YEC: Objections – Too Much for Day 6

![](pics/AdamNamingAnimals.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **Adam could not name all the animals in one day** → only livestock, birds, and beasts of the field – a few hundred kinds, an hour or two
* **"At last" (Genesis 2:23 RSV) means a long wait** → it marks the end of the search, not a span (NKJV "This is now"): after the animals, finally a fitting partner

---

## YEC: The Cost of Dropping It

![](pics/SmithTwoTowers.png style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Every worldview is a tower of supporting beliefs – a tile bears weight only if it is **defensible from the foundation** (adapted from Calvin Smith)
* The Christian tower rises from Genesis – Creation, Fall, Flood, Law – to Virgin Birth, **Cross**, Resurrection – and to Restoration
* The atheist tower rises from evolution to heat death
* "Science must be allowed to modify our exegesis of Scripture" (Davis Young, geologist at the conservative Reformed Calvin College)
  * YEC: if allowed for Genesis, then also for the New Testament and the accounts of Jesus

---

## YEC: The Cross Left Alone

![](pics/SmithCrossAlone.png style="float: right; width: 7rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"For **as in Adam all die**, even so **in Christ all shall be made alive**."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>1 Corinthians 15:22 (NKJV)</em></p>

* Applied tile by tile, science-led exegesis reinterprets, or calls myth, **every tile but the Cross**: Creation, Fall, Flood, Law, Virgin Birth, Resurrection, Restoration
* The core of the gospel is not touched – but its **defensibility** is
* The Cross now stands on nothing: an **unsupported belief**
* The atheist's question: you gave up everything else in the Bible when science objected – how can you be sure about the cross?
* Paul ties the cross to Genesis: either the first Adam and the last Adam are **both real history, or neither is**

---

## YEC – Polishing

![](pics/PolishingDiamond.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **Nothing essential is cut** – the timeline stands
* Only **over-reach** is cut:
  * Aligning the text with a scientific model risks concordism, so hold it **tentatively**:
    * Reliable so far: a rotating Earth from evening and morning
    * Weaker: an ongoing cosmic expansion from "stretches out the heavens"
    * Abandoned: the vapor canopy from "the waters above"
  * Treating fellow Christians with old-earth views as heretics – the name-calling went both ways; answer "with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15), with gentleness and respect

---

## Literal Naive

![](pics/MosesLimitedScience.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* God created in six 24-hour days, and Moses **described it with the limited science of his time** (von Rad, Pannenberg)
* The account "is completely bound to the cosmological knowledge of its time" and means to convey it – a theology that used the science it had (von Rad)
* Its strength: it hears the text honestly – the author **meant ordinary days**
* No professor of Hebrew "at any world-class university" doubts the writer meant six days "of 24 hours" – a hostile witness: James Barr, Oxford professor of Hebrew and no believer in Genesis (letter, 1984)
* Facet kept: **the author meant ordinary days**

---

## Literal Naive – Cutting

![](pics/BibleInspiration.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* If the account is in Moses' own science, it is Moses' account – but no human was there: Genesis 1 only makes sense as **God's account, revealed**, and God has no obsolete science
* The "obsolete" features dissolve on inspection: the firmament (raqia) is "the whole sky" (Craig, Excursus 19) – birds fly across it, lights move in it
* Cut away: **"Moses described it with obsolete science"** – the text is God's account

---

## Verse 1–2 Gap

![](pics/RuinedPreAdamicEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2:
  * **Billions of years** and a pre-Adamic world
  * The fall of Satan
  * A judgment ("Lucifer's flood") that left the earth ruined and empty – the gap reading of "without form and void"
* Proponents: Thomas Chalmers (1814), Scofield Reference Bible (1909)

---

## Verse 1–2 Gap: Its Reasons

![](pics/EarthBecameTohu.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Why read a gap into the text?
  * The verb hayah is read as "the earth **became** without form and void"
  * Isaiah 45:18: God "did not create it in vain (tohu)" – so, they argue, it must have **become** tohu
  * Fossil death cannot follow a "very good" creation – so it must come **before** it (Pember)
* Then six literal 24-hour days of re-creation – explaining the fossils within a literal week
* Facet kept: **Genesis 1:2 describes a real first state** – "without form and void": unformed and unfilled, uninhabited (see [Creation out of Nothing](Creation.Slides.1.CreationOutOfNothing.html))

---

## Verse 1–2 Gap – Cutting

![](pics/UnformedNotRuined.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Verse 2 is a circumstantial clause – "Now the earth **was**", not "**became**" (AiG; Craig agrees on the clause, though he leaves a bare time gap before verse 3 open)
* Exodus 20:11: the heavens and the earth were made **in six days** – no ages before them
* Mark 10:6: man and woman "from the beginning of the creation" – not at the end of a billions-of-years timeline (slide 17)
* A prior life world is "utterly foreign to the text" – "concordism at its very worst" (Craig, Excursus 4)
* Cut away: **a pre-Adamic world, death, and a fallen Satan before "very good"** (Genesis 1:31)

---

## Day-Gap

![](pics/LongAgesClock.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Six real 24-hour days of creative acts, **separated by long ages** in which things develop
* Takes the days seriously as days
* Takes God's direct creative acts seriously
* Facet kept: **real days of divine action**
* Proponents: John Lennox, *Seven Days That Divide the World*

---

## Day-Gap – Cutting

![](pics/ConsecutiveDaysNoGap.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* "Evening and morning": each day ends and the next begins – **no room for a gap** (Craig, Excursus 4)
* Nothing in the local or wider biblical context marks gaps; Lennox does not defend them from the text
* It does not even fit the fossil dates: birds (day 5) before land animals (day 6)
* Ages of developing life between the days imply death before "very good" – which contradicts Genesis 1:31 and Romans 5:12 (slides 14, 19)
* Cut away: **ages inserted between the days**

---

## Day-Age

![](pics/day-age-timeline.png style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Six consecutive **long ages** matching the secular scientific timeline (Hugh Ross, Reasons to Believe)
* Yom **can** mean a long period; day 7 is still going on; day 6 seems too full for 24 hours
* Ross loves God's Word and God's world, and RTB reaches people who struggle with young-earth views
* Facet kept: **a real sequence of creative acts** – God really made, in order

---

## Day-Age: The Official Timeline

![](pics/DayAgeOfficialTimeLine.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Day 1: 14–3 billion years ago – sun, moon, stars; first single-celled life
* Day 2: stable water cycle
* Day 3: all green plants (to 700 million years ago)
* Day 4: clouds clear, the sun becomes **visible**
* Day 5: fish and birds
* Day 6: mammals, Adam and Eve (200,000 to 50,000 years ago)
* Day 7: 50,000 years ago until today – God still resting

---

## Day-Age – Cutting: Yom

![](pics/YomStretched.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Yom **can** stretch – but can it stretch **here**? Number + evening + morning, six times
* "In my father's day, he spent six days crossing the country" – context decides, and Genesis 1 decides for 24 hours
* Yom here means a 24-hour day, "It does not mean 'age'" – "but the 24 hour days could be" a figure for long ages (Craig agrees, Excursus 5); yet the days sit inside the narrative, so the narrative claims them (Where Is the Vehicle?)
* Day 7 does not continue (slides 14, 39); day 6 is not too full (slide 41)
* If only modern science can reveal the meaning of Genesis, then Moses, David, and the apostles could not have understood it

---

## Day-Age – Cutting: The Days

![](pics/DayAgeOfficialTimeLine.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* **Day 1:** sun and stars and first life are read into "the heavens and the earth"; rachaph (hovering) does not mean creating life
* **Day 2:** hundreds of millions of years of rain, never once clear? And "no rain" (Genesis 2:5)
* **Day 3:** flowering plants appear in the fossils 130 million years ago; who pollinates without animals? A day without the sun is no problem for plants; an age is
* **Day 4:** verses 14–15 on day 4, verse 16 on day 1, verses 17–18 on day 4?
* **Overlapping days:** contradicts evening-morning and the Exodus work week

---

## Day-Age – Cutting: Death

![](pics/CatEatsMouse.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Millions of years of **predation, disease, and extinction** – then called "very good" (Genesis 1:31)?
* Plant food for man and beast (Genesis 1:29–30); meat only in Genesis 9:3 – fossil predators must come **after** the fall
* Fossil **thorns** are dated long before man – but thorns came with the curse (Genesis 3:18)
* Death is the penalty for sin (Romans 5:12) – not a tool of creation
* The curse on "every beast" (Genesis 3:14): on day-age, what changed? Nothing

---

## Day-Age – Cutting: The Arithmetic

![](pics/UssherBibleTimeline.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Day-age puts Adam 50,000 to 200,000 years ago – up to **194,000 extra years** over the Bible's chronology
* Not from Adam to Joseph: every father's age is given
* Not since Jesus. So between Joseph and Jesus?
* Not Joseph to Jesus either: 430 years in Egypt (Exodus 12:40), the temple begun 480 years after the Exodus in about 966 BC (1 Kings 6:1) – Jacob's family entered Egypt about 1876 BC
* From Adam to Jesus the Bible counts the years – no room for 194,000 more
* Cut away: **ages instead of days** – concordism

---

## Revelation-Day

![](pics/MosesReceivingDays.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* God created over long ages; then over **six literal days He revealed** to Moses what He had done, one day's account per day
* Related: **days of divine fiat** – six days of proclamation, fulfilled later over ages
* Its strength: it answers **how Moses knew** – no human was there, so God must have revealed it
* Facet kept: **God revealed the account** – Moses was not there

---

## Revelation-Day – Cutting

![](pics/GodSpheresOfPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* The proclamations **presuppose** what came before – "Let the earth bring forth" needs dry land (Craig, Excursus 5)
* "God saw that it was good" means the work was **done** that day, not ages later
* Exodus 20:11: "in six days the LORD **made**" – not "told"
* Cut away: **the days as days of telling instead of days of making**

---

## Literary Framework

![](pics/FormingFilling.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* The days are a **literary frame**, not a chronology (Henri Blocher, Meredith Kline)
* Days 1–3 **form** the realms (light, sky and sea, land); days 4–6 **fill** them (lights, birds and fish, animals and man)
* A real pattern, noticed since the Middle Ages
* Day 4 describes the **same event** as day 1 in more detail – so the days cannot be a sequence (Kline)
* Facet kept: **structure – forming (days 1–3) and filling (days 4–6)**

---

## Literary Framework – Cutting

![](pics/ChronoLogical.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* The pairs are not exact: the lights of day 4 are set in the firmament of **day 2**; sea creatures fill the seas of **day 3** (Craig, Excursus 6)
* Ordinal numbers and the rise from desolation to man **suggest chronology** (Craig)
* "Good" marks each work's **completion** in sequence; day 4 does not replay day 1 (CMI)
* Exodus 20:8–11: the Sabbath rests **on** the history, not the history on the Sabbath
* Cut away: **structure instead of sequence** – a well-built history is still history

---

## Functional Creation (FC)

![](pics/HeavenCity.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Ancient readers thought of creation as **giving function**: like a building that becomes a restaurant when it opens
* Seven literal days **inaugurate the cosmos as God's temple**; on day 7 God takes up residence
* It reminds us that God had a **purpose** for everything He made
* Facet kept: **purpose – creation as God's temple, ordered for function**
* Proponents: John Walton, *The Lost World of Genesis One*

---

## FC – Cutting: The Things Made

![](pics/CosmosTemple.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Lego: pieces (material cause), maker (efficient), blueprint (formal), a home for Lego people (final cause) – **all four**, not only the last
* The objects of **bara** in Genesis 1 – heavens and earth, sea creatures, man – are material things that come into being when created (Craig, Excursus 7)
* On Walton's view the land, plants, fish, birds, animals, and man were **not made** in the creation week
* What would an eyewitness have seen that week? Walton admits the world lacked only humans in God's image and God's presence – everything else looked the same (Craig, Excursus 7)

---

## FC – Cutting: Rest, Not Residence

![](pics/CosmosTemple.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* On Walton's reading the week only assigned functions to things already there – so "Let there be light", "let the dry land appear", "let the earth bring forth" describe no event: every such statement is **literally false** (Craig, Excursus 7)
* The purely functional reading is therefore "implausible (not to say ridiculous)" (Craig)
* Day 7: if God made nothing in six days, He has nothing to **rest** from – so Walton must turn resting into **residing** in His temple
* But Exodus 20:11 grounds our Sabbath in God's rest from **work** done in six days; "reside" is read into the text (Craig, Excursus 8)
* Cut away: **function instead of material origins**

---

## Creation Myth

![](pics/Pyramids.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Genesis recasts Egyptian creation stories to **correct their theology**:
  * God is uncreated, alone, sovereign over all – no other gods
  * Man, not the sun, is the crown of creation – God's image
  * Weekly Sabbath rest instead of the sun god's daily fight with chaos
* Facet kept: **polemic** – a pointed attack on the rival stories: **Genesis answers the ancient Near Eastern myths**
* Proponents: Miller and Soden, *In the Beginning … We Misunderstood* (Craig, Excursus 9)

---

## Creation Myth – Cutting

![](pics/TrueStoryVsMyth.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Miller and Soden admit the "Egyptian myth" is a mosaic they had to assemble from bits across 2,000 years of inscriptions (Craig, Excursus 9)
* "Parallelomania": parallels must be real, causal, and one-directional – rarely shown (Craig, Excursus 10–12)
* A polemic against false stories **works only if the true story is true** (CMI)
* Genesis has neither **theogony** (how the gods were born) nor **theomachy** (war among the gods) – the engines of the Egyptian and Babylonian stories (Craig)
* "Myth instead of history" would also remove the historical Adam (slide 78)
* Cut away: **myth instead of history**

---

## Mytho-History

![](pics/CoffeeAndCream.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Genesis 1–11 is **myth** in the scholar's sense – a traditional sacred narrative that anchors present realities in the primeval past (Craig, Excursus 13–21)
* But with a real **interest in history**: the genealogies arrange it on a line of time (Jacobsen; Wenham: "proto-history")
* Real people and events "clothed in the metaphorical language of myth" – blended like **coffee and cream**, not sortable like marbles in a bag: which parts are which cannot be told apart
* Adam and his sin are **historical** (Romans 5) – Craig affirms this firmly

---

## Mytho-History: The Closest Rival

![](pics/CoffeeAndCream.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* It sees the **figurative layer** truly: anthropomorphisms, the trees, the rib, the days carry real meaning
* The **genealogies anchor** the history – real people who lived and died
* Its central truths are ours too (slide 80)
* Facet kept: **the figurative layer – and the genealogical anchor**
* The **closest rival** to the diamond

---

## Mytho-History: Craig's Case

![](pics/CoffeeAndCream.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Genesis 1–11 shares with the myths their **themes and etiological motifs** – present realities explained by primeval events (Excursus 13)
* Figurative language is **true without being literal**: "It's raining cats and dogs" (Excursus 17)
* Myths are **plastic** (they vary between tellers) and **flexible** (they change over time) – so their societies did not take them literalistically (Excursus 17–18)
* Some features seem "palpably false" if literal: a talking snake, fruit that gives knowledge, a rainless earth watered by a mist (Excursus 19–20)
* So: real history in the figurative dress of myth – now we test whether the dress removes the referents

---

## Mytho-History – Polishing: The Referents

![](pics/EveReachingForFruit.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Features read as figurative because they seem **"palpably false"** if literal (Craig, Excursus 19–20) – but each has a referent:
  * **Trees:** the tree of life returns, real, in Revelation 2:7; 22:2
  * **Serpent:** "that serpent of old, called the Devil and Satan" (Revelation 12:9)
  * **Cherubim:** real beings – in the tabernacle (Exodus 25), seen by Ezekiel (Ezekiel 1; 10)
  * **God walking:** real closeness in a real place
  * **Days:** a real week behind the Sabbath pattern (Exodus 20:11)

---

## Mytho-History – Polishing: Genesis 2

![](pics/TreeDryLush.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **A rainless Earth "fantastic"?** The text supplies how it was watered – "a mist went up" (Genesis 2:6)
* **Genesis 1 and 2 inconsistent?** Genesis 2 **zooms in on day 6**: a garden, a man, a naming, a woman – a close-up, not a second order
* **Plants (2:5):** "shrub and grain of the field = cultivated plants" is too clever by half (Craig, Excursus 20) – but 2:5 states conditions (no rain, no tiller), not causes: the garden is planted for the man (2:8)
* **Animals (2:19):** "formed" can be pluperfect (NIV "had formed"); only beasts of the field and birds are brought to be named

---

## Mytho-History – Polishing: Lifespans

![](pics/ScrollWithGoldLetters.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Add up the years and Noah is alive when Abraham is born; Shem outlives Abraham – "seems crazy" (Craig, Excursus 15)
* That is exactly what Ussher's chart shows, and nothing in the text objects – incredulity, not exegesis
* Counted years with fathers' ages are chronicle style, not myth – even Jacobsen marks numbers as the historian's interest
* The 10 + 10 symmetry (Sarna) is the author's arrangement of real names – Jude 14 still counts Enoch "the seventh from Adam"

---

## Mytho-History – Polishing: "Fantastic"

![](pics/GodUnlimitedPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* The fruit are not miraculous – they work "even contrary to God's will" (Craig, Excursus 19) → then a **created** property, like bread nourishing or hemlock killing; God's command opposed the eating, not the tree's working
* The knowledge of good and evil came from the **act** of disobedience, not from chemistry (Genesis 3:7)
* God guards the tree of life because it is real and works (Genesis 3:22–24) – and it returns "for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2)
* A real tree of life is **no harder** for the Creator than a real Adam

---

## Mytho-History – Polishing: The Verdict

![](pics/GodUnlimitedPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* **The talking serpent:** a miracle here would make God "the author of the Fall" (Craig) – but a creature used by a fallen creature is no miracle of God (compare Judas, John 13:27)
* Deciding what God may have done by what seems plausible is **concordism in reverse** – modern plausibility read into the text
* Genre is found from the text: Genesis 1–11 uses the same narrative form as Genesis 12–50
* Polished off: **"figurative, so it did not happen"** – the referents stay; only the "palpably false" test goes

---

## Ten Views Cut – Day = 24 Hours

![](pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg style="float: right; width: 15rem")

* **YEC**: the historical core, kept whole – only over-reach polished off
* **Literal Naive**: the author meant days – "obsolete science" cut
* **Verse 1–2 Gap**: a real first state – a pre-Adamic world cut
* **Day-Gap**: real days of divine action – inserted ages cut
* **Revelation-Day**: God revealed it – days of telling cut

## Ten Views Cut – Day ≠ 24 Hours

![](pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg style="float: right; width: 15rem")

* **Day-Age**: a real sequence of creative acts – ages for days cut
* **Literary Framework**: forming and filling – "instead of sequence" cut
* **Functional Creation**: purpose – "no material origins" cut
* **Creation Myth**: the polemic – "myth instead of history" cut
* **Mytho-History**: the figurative layer – its referents restored

* **Core:** six real 24-hour days, a real Adam, a real fall, a real Flood
