## Where We Are: From Creation to the Days

![](pics/GodCreation.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Deck 1: God created **everything** out of nothing – Genesis 1:1
* Now the question that divides Christians: **how** and **how long**?
* Six days of 24 hours, or long ages, or no timeline at all?

* This is not a question to settle before coming to Christ

# How to Read Genesis

---

## Exegesis, Eisegesis, Concordism

![](pics/LightOnWords.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **Exegesis** – finding what the text means on its own
* **Eisegesis** – adding one's own ideas into the text
* **Concordism** – reading modern science back into the text
* The question is always: how would the **author and his first audience** have understood it?
* Rule of thumb: "When the plain sense makes common sense, seek no other sense, lest it be nonsense"

---

## Genre: History with Artful Structure

![](pics/ScrollWithGoldLetters.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Genesis has historical account, careful wording, high structure, and symbolic meaning – **all in chapter 1**
* **Historical:** Adam and Eve were real people, with a lineage all the way to Jesus; God really made all things in the creation week
* **Symbolic:** Adam = "man", Eve = "mother of all living", God breathing into Adam, God walking with Adam
* Genesis 1 uses the Hebrew narrative verb form (waw-consecutive) of history, not the parallelism of Hebrew poetry (CMI)

---

## The Cutting Tools

![](pics/EyeOverFork.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **(T1) Exegesis:** does it come **from the text**, as the author and his first audience would have read it?
* **(T2) Compatibility:** can it stand **alongside the historical timeline**, or must it replace it?
* A view that passes both is a true facet
* A view that fails T1 is read into the text; a view that fails T2 cuts into the core
* And a third tool, often overlooked …

---

## Every Figure Has a Referent

![](pics/BookThink.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* **(T3)** A figurative meaning **rides on something concrete** – calling a feature figurative does not remove the thing it refers to
* Aquinas: all the senses of Scripture are "founded on the literal" (*Summa Theologiae* I, q.1, a.10)
* Augustine: every sign (*signum*) points to a thing (*res*)
* Two kinds of figure in Genesis 1–3 – and neither empties the history

---

## Figurative Words, Real Events

![](pics/TakingAwalk.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* The **words** are figurative, the **event** is real:
* "The LORD God walking in the garden" (Genesis 3:8) → real daily closeness and conversation between God and Adam, in a real place
* "Formed from the dust" (Genesis 2:7) → a body really made of the earth – "to dust you shall return" (Genesis 3:19)
* An anthropomorphism is a figure **of something** – not a figure **of nothing**

---

## Real Things with Figurative Meaning

![](pics/EveReachingForFruit.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Typology: the **thing itself** is the referent, and it also means more:
  * **The two trees** – a moral law carried by a concrete prohibition
  * **The rib** – "bone of my bones" (Genesis 2:23): one flesh in marriage
  * **The serpent** – the real deceiver behind it (Revelation 12:9)
  * **The cherubim** – real guardians of God's holiness
  * **The six days** – Exodus 20:11 needs a real week behind the pattern

---

## Even the Rock Is Real

![](pics/ImmutableRock.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"**The LORD is my rock** and my fortress and my deliverer; My God, my strength, in whom I will trust; My shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Psalm 18:2 (NKJV)</em></p>

* A pure metaphor – yet it needs a **real rock**: big, heavy, not moved by wind, rain, or an angry man, a foundation to build on (Matthew 7:24–25)
* That solidity and reliability God **truly has** – no exceptions

---

## Where Is the Vehicle?

![](pics/TreeDryLush.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* What differs is **where the vehicle is**:
  * In a psalm the rock is out in the world – **no event with a rock** is narrated
  * In Genesis 2–3 the trees, the serpent, and the days are placed **inside the story**
* When the vehicle is an object or event **of the narrative**, the narrative claims it existed
* So "figurative" can add meaning to the history – it cannot subtract the history

## Day One: Evening and Morning

![](pics/Sunset.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

<p style="font-family: 'EB Garamond', serif; font-style: italic;">"God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. **So the evening and the morning were the first day**."</p><p style="text-align: right; margin-top: -1.25em; margin-bottom: -0.75em;"> <em>Genesis 1:5 (NKJV)</em></p>

# Cutting the Facets

---

## How Each View Is Cut

![](pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg style="float: right; width: 15rem")

* Ten views, one after another – for each:
  * **"The Facet"** – the view stated fairly, its strengths, the true facet it adds
  * **"Polishing the Facet"** – its weaknesses, tested with T1, T2, T3 and the texts we just read; cut away
* We start with the core and end with the closest rival
* Each view corrects an error in science, in theology, or in both (slides 3–4) – we keep what is right and build the best of all

---

## Literal Accurate (YEC): The Facet

![](pics/JesusHoldingEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* God created everything in **six consecutive 24-hour days**, then rested one day
* Moses recorded **God's historically accurate account**, as God revealed it to him
* **Yom with a number**: 359 times in the Old Testament outside Genesis – always an ordinary day
* **Yom with evening and morning**: always an ordinary day
* Exodus 20:11: the same six days ground our work week
* Facet kept: **the historical timeline – the core**

---

## Literal Accurate (YEC): The Facet (2)

![](pics/JesusHoldingEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Hebrew had other words for long ages (e.g. **olam**) – the author did not use them
* The plain reading was the church's standard reading: Basil, Luther, Calvin; Augustine made creation **instant**, not long
* No long-age reading of the days arises from the text before the rise of old-earth geology (around 1800)
* Two meanings at once: the **historical account** and the **pattern for our week**

---

## YEC: Polishing the Facet – Craig's Objections

![](pics/Arguments.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Craig (Excursus 3), fairly: "a perfectly feasible interpretation" – but not the only one. His reasons:
  * "In the day" (Genesis 2:4) uses yom broadly → yes, as the idiom "when" – no number, no evening-morning
  * Hosea 6:2 "third day" is non-literal → prophetic poetry; and its days still refer to real, short spans
  * "The arm of the LORD" is a metaphor → yes, but a metaphor needs a referent (T3): Exodus 20:11 points to a real week
  * Day 7 has no evening → God's rest from creating continues; the day still anchors a 24-hour Sabbath

---

## YEC: Polishing the Facet – More Objections

![](pics/Arguments.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* **No sun until day 4** → God made light on day 1; the text itself has day and night before the sun – no concordism needed
* **Trees in one day** → "Let the earth bring forth" is a creative command, like "Let there be"; God made mature trees as He made a mature Adam
* **Adam naming the animals** → livestock, birds, and beasts of the field – a few hundred kinds, an hour or two
* "At last" (Genesis 2:23 RSV; NKJV "This is now") → after the animals, finally a fitting partner

---

## YEC: What Is Cut Away

![](pics/ArrogantScientist.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* **Nothing essential** – the timeline stands
* Only **over-reach** is cut:
  * Claiming the text teaches modern science (Earth's rotation, a vapor canopy) – that is concordism too
  * Treating fellow Christians with old-earth views as heretics
* Name-calling went both ways; both sides should answer "with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15)

---

## Literal Naive: The Facet

![](pics/AncientKing.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* God created in six literal 24-hour days, and Moses **described it with the limited science of his time**
* Its strength: it hears the text honestly – the author **meant ordinary days**
* Many scholars who do not believe Genesis still agree: the author intended six ordinary days
* Facet kept: **the author meant ordinary days**

---

## Literal Naive: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/BibleInspiration.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Moses was not there – how could he describe creation in his own words?
* Genesis 1 only makes sense as **God's account, revealed** – and God has no obsolete science
* The "obsolete" features dissolve on inspection: the firmament (raqia) is "the whole sky" – birds fly across it, lights move in it (Craig, Excursus 19)
* Cut away: **"Moses described it with obsolete science"** – the text is God's account

---

## Verse 1–2 Gap: The Facet

![](pics/RosetteNebula.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Thomas Chalmers (1814), Scofield Reference Bible (1909)
* Between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2: **billions of years**, a pre-Adamic world, the fall of Satan, a judgment ("Lucifer's flood")
* Then six literal 24-hour days of re-creation – explaining the fossils within a literal week
* Facet kept: **Genesis 1:2 describes a real first state** – "without form and void": unformed and unfilled, uninhabited (Deck 1)

---

## Verse 1–2 Gap: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/Lightning.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Verse 2 is a circumstantial clause – "Now the earth **was**", not "**became**" (AiG); Craig: a prior life world is "utterly foreign to the text", though he leaves a bare time gap before verse 3 open
* Exodus 20:11: the heavens and the earth were made **in six days** – no ages before them
* Craig: a prior life world is "utterly foreign to the text" – "concordism at its very worst" (Excursus 4)
* Cut away: **a pre-Adamic world, death, and a fallen Satan before "very good"** (Genesis 1:31)

---

## Day-Gap: The Facet

![](pics/GodCosmosClock.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* John Lennox, *Seven Days That Divide the World*
* Six real 24-hour days of creative acts, **separated by long ages** in which things develop
* Takes the days seriously as days, and takes God's direct acts seriously
* Facet kept: **real days of divine action**

---

## Day-Gap: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/Dominos.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* "Evening and morning": each day ends and the next begins – **no room for a gap** (Craig, Excursus 4)
* Nothing in the local or wider biblical context marks gaps; Lennox does not defend them from the text
* It does not even fit the fossil dates: birds (day 5) before land animals (day 6)
* Cut away: **ages inserted between the days** – and with them death before "very good"

---

## Day-Age: The Facet

![](pics/day-age-timeline.png style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Hugh Ross / Reasons to Believe: six consecutive **long ages** matching the scientific timeline
* Yom **can** mean a long period; day 7 is still going on; day 6 seems too full for 24 hours
* Ross loves God's Word and God's world, and RTB reaches people who struggle with young-earth views
* Facet kept: **a real sequence of creative acts** – God really made, in order

---

## Day-Age: The Official Timeline

![](pics/DayAgeOfficialTimeLine.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Day 1: 14–3 billion years ago – sun, moon, stars; first single-celled life
* Day 2: stable water cycle; day 3: all green plants (to 700 million years ago)
* Day 4: clouds clear, the sun becomes **visible**
* Day 5: fish and birds; day 6: mammals, Adam and Eve (to 50,000 years ago)
* Day 7: 50,000 years ago until today – God still resting

---

## Day-Age: Polishing the Facet – Yom

![](pics/FatManSpandexYomDay1.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* Yom **can** stretch – but can it stretch **here**? Number + evening + morning, 6 times
* "In my father's day, he spent six days crossing the country" – context decides, and Genesis 1 decides for 24 hours
* Craig agrees: yom here means a 24-hour day, "It does not mean 'age'" – "but the 24 hour days could be" a figure for long ages (Excursus 5); yet the days sit inside the narrative, so the narrative claims them (Where Is the Vehicle?)
* Day 7 does not continue: Exodus 20:11 commands **one day** of rest, not an unending one
* If only modern science can reveal Genesis, Moses, David, and the apostles could not understand it

---

## Day-Age: Polishing the Facet – The Days

![](pics/DayAgeOfficialTimeLine.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* **Day 1:** sun and stars and first life are read into "the heavens and the earth"; rachaph (hovering) does not mean creating life
* **Day 2:** hundreds of millions of years of rain, never once clear? And "no rain" (Genesis 2:5)
* **Day 3:** flowering plants appear in the fossils 130 million years ago; who pollinates without animals?
* **Day 4:** verses 14–15 on day 4, verse 16 on day 1, verses 17–18 on day 4?
* **Overlapping days:** contradicts evening-morning and the Exodus work week

---

## Day-Age: Polishing the Facet – Death

![](pics/CatEatsMouse.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Millions of years of **predation, disease, and extinction** – then called "very good" (Genesis 1:31)?
* Plant food for man and beast (Genesis 1:29–30); meat only in Genesis 9:3 – fossil predators must come **after** the fall
* Fossil **thorns** are dated long before man – but thorns came with the curse (Genesis 3:18)
* Death is the penalty for sin (Romans 5:12) – not a tool of creation
* The curse on "every beast" (Genesis 3:14): on day-age, what changed? Nothing

---

## Day-Age: Polishing the Facet – The Arithmetic

![](pics/UssherBibleTimeline.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Day-age puts Adam 50,000 or more years ago – up to **194,000 extra years** over the Bible's chronology
* Not from Adam to Joseph: every father's age is given
* Not since Jesus. So between Joseph and Jesus?
* Joseph was in Egypt, and even secular archaeology has no Nile settlements before about 6000 BC – room for 2,000 years at most
* The generations from Joseph to Jesus (Matthew 1) at 45 years each put Joseph near 1700 BC
* Cut away: **yom stretched to ages – concordism; death before the fall**

---

## Revelation-Day: The Facet

![](pics/DreamVision.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* God created over long ages; then over **six literal days He revealed** to Moses what He had done, one day's account per day
* Related: **days of divine fiat** – six days of proclamation, fulfilled later over ages
* Its strength: it asks **how Moses knew** – no human was there
* Facet kept: **God revealed the account** – Moses was not there

---

## Revelation-Day: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/GodSpheresOfPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Craig (Excursus 5): the proclamations **presuppose** what came before – "Let the earth bring forth" needs dry land
* "God saw that it was good" means the work was **done** that day, not ages later
* Exodus 20:11: "in six days the LORD **made**" – not "told"
* Cut away: **the days as days of telling instead of days of making**

---

## Literary Framework: The Facet

![](pics/GodsToys.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Henri Blocher, Meredith Kline: the days are a **literary frame**, not a chronology
* Days 1–3 **form** the realms (light, sky and sea, land); days 4–6 **fill** them (lights, birds and fish, animals and man)
* A real pattern, noticed since the Middle Ages
* Facet kept: **structure – forming (days 1–3) and filling (days 4–6)**

---

## Literary Framework: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/ChronoLogical.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* The pairs are not exact: the lights of day 4 are set in the firmament of **day 2**; sea creatures fill the seas of **day 3** (Craig, Excursus 6)
* Ordinal numbers and the rise from desolation to man **suggest chronology** (Craig)
* "Good" marks each work's **completion** in sequence; day 4 does not replay day 1 (CMI)
* Exodus 20:8–11: the Sabbath rests **on** the history, not the history on the Sabbath
* Cut away: **structure instead of sequence** – a well-built history is still history

---

## Functional Creation: The Facet

![](pics/HeavenCity.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

* John Walton, *The Lost World of Genesis One*
* Ancient readers thought of creation as **giving function**: like a building that becomes a restaurant when it opens
* Seven literal days **inaugurate the cosmos as God's temple**; on day 7 God takes up residence
* It reminds us that God had a **purpose** for everything He made
* Facet kept: **purpose – creation as God's temple, ordered for function**

---

## Functional Creation: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/GenRevFunctions.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Lego: pieces (material cause), maker (efficient), blueprint (formal), a home for Lego people (final cause) – **all four**, not only the last
* **Bara** means God as efficient cause – birds and sea creatures are made on day 5 (Craig, Excursus 8)
* On Walton's view the land, plants, fish, birds, animals, and man were **not made** in the creation week
* Craig: purely functional reading "implausible (not to say ridiculous)" – it would make every statement about darkness, sea, dry land, and plants literally false; the seven days follow the Sabbath, not a temple dedication
* Cut away: **function instead of material origins**

---

## Creation Myth: The Facet

![](pics/Pyramids.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Miller and Soden, *In the Beginning … We Misunderstood* (Craig, Excursus 9)
* Genesis recasts Egyptian creation stories to **correct their theology**:
  * God is uncreated, alone, sovereign over all – no other gods
  * Man, not the sun, is the crown of creation – God's image
  * Weekly Sabbath rest instead of the sun god's daily fight with chaos
* Facet kept: **polemic – Genesis answers the ancient Near Eastern myths**

---

## Creation Myth: Polishing the Facet

![](pics/GreekPhilosophy.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* Miller and Soden admit the "Egyptian myth" is a mosaic they had to assemble from bits across 2,000 years of inscriptions (Craig, Excursus 9)
* "Parallelomania": parallels must be real, causal, and one-directional – rarely shown (Craig, Excursus 10–12)
* A polemic against false stories **works only if the true story is true** (CMI)
* Genesis 1:1 is no title but an independent sentence (Deck 1)
* Cut away: **myth instead of history** – and with it the historical Adam

---

## Mytho-History: The Facet

![](pics/ScrollsReview.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Craig (Excursus 13–21): Genesis 1–11 is **myth** in the scholar's sense – a traditional sacred narrative that anchors present realities in the primeval past
* But with a real **interest in history**: the genealogies arrange it on a line of time (Jacobsen; Wenham: "proto-history")
* Real people and events "clothed in the metaphorical language of myth" – "coffee and cream"
* Adam and his sin are **historical** (Romans 5) – Craig affirms this firmly

---

## Mytho-History: The Facet (2)

![](pics/ScrollsReview.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* It sees the **figurative layer** truly: anthropomorphisms, the trees, the rib, the days carry real meaning
* The **genealogies anchor** the history – real people who lived and died
* Its central truths are ours too (see "The Finished Diamond")
* Facet kept: **the figurative layer – and the genealogical anchor**
* The **closest rival** to the diamond

---

## Mytho-History: Polishing the Facet – The Referents

![](pics/EveReachingForFruit.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Craig reads features as figurative because they seem **"palpably false"** if literal (Excursus 19–20) – but each has a referent (T3):
  * **Trees:** the tree of life returns, real, in Revelation 2:7; 22:2
  * **Serpent:** "that serpent of old, called the Devil and Satan" (Revelation 12:9)
  * **Cherubim:** real beings – in the tabernacle (Exodus 25), seen by Ezekiel (Ezekiel 1; 10)
  * **God walking:** real closeness in a real place
  * **Days:** Exodus 20:11

---

## Mytho-History: Polishing the Facet – Genesis 2

![](pics/TreeDryLush.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **A rainless Earth "fantastic"?** The text supplies how it was watered – "a mist went up" (Genesis 2:6)
* **Genesis 1 and 2 inconsistent?** Genesis 2 **zooms in on day 6**: plants "of the field" for tilling, a garden, a man, a naming, a woman
* Genesis 2 is not a second creation order but a close-up of the one day
* **Long lifespans:** counted years with fathers' ages are chronicle style, not myth – even Jacobsen marks numbers as the historian's interest

---

## Mytho-History: Polishing the Facet – "Fantastic"

![](pics/GodUmlimitedPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

* "Fantastic" here means **"miraculous"**
* The Creator of the universe out of nothing makes a real tree of life **no harder** than a real Adam
* Deciding what God may have done by what seems plausible is **concordism in reverse** – modern plausibility read into the text
* Genre is found from the text: Genesis 1–11 uses the same narrative form as Genesis 12–50
* Cut away: **the referents** – events treated as figurative because they seem "palpably false"

# The Finished Diamond

---

## Adam in the New Testament

![](pics/JesusReadingScrolls.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Jesus: "from the beginning of the creation" (Mark 10:6) – marriage grounded in the first pair
* Luke 3 traces Jesus' genealogy back to Adam; "from one blood" all nations (Acts 17:26)
* Paul: through one man sin and death entered (Romans 5:12); "as in Adam all die" (1 Corinthians 15:22)
* Craig: an act inside a fiction cannot have effects outside it – so Adam is **historical** (Man 14)
* Jesus and Paul read Genesis as **history**

---

## The Polished Diamond

![](pics/diamond.png style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* **Core:** six real 24-hour days, a real Adam, a real fall, a real Flood
* **Structure:** forming and filling (Framework)
* **Purpose:** the cosmos as God's temple (Walton)
* **Polemic:** God alone, against the myths (Miller and Soden)
* **Figure:** the trees, the rib, the days carry meaning (Craig) – every figure with its referent
* **Revelation, sequence, first state** (Revelation-Day, Day-Age, Gap) – **Moral:** the Sabbath

---

## Central Truths of Genesis 1–11

![](pics/GodCreated.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

* Craig's ten truths (Excursus 21) – common ground with other Christians:
  * God is one, the transcendent Creator; He designed the world and its life
  * Man is the pinnacle of creation, able to know God; male and female, equal, made for marriage
  * Work is good; human discovery is our God-given calling – the root of science
  * One day in seven is holy
  * The first pair freely sinned; sin spreads and brings judgment
  * Yet God's purpose to bless all mankind stands

# Practical Takeaways

---

## Talking with Old-Earth Christians

![](pics/coffeeTalk.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Affirm **the facet they see** before showing what is cut
  * Framework: "Yes, the structure is beautiful – and it is history"
  * Walton: "Yes, God had a purpose – and He also made the things"
  * Ross: "Yes, God made in order – and in days"
* They are brothers and sisters; this is not a gospel issue to settle first
* Avoid starting here with non-Christians – start with Christ

---

## Practical Takeaways

![](pics/diamond.png style="float: right; width: 10rem")

* Read **out of** the text, not into it – neither science nor plausibility gets the first word
* Every figure has a **referent** – "figurative" never means "did not happen"
* The six days ground **our week**: work six, rest one – worship the Creator every Sabbath
* Death is an **enemy** from the fall, not a tool of creation – and Christ has defeated it
* Genesis is the foundation of the gospel: a real Adam needs a real Last Adam
