# Creation Unit – Structure Plan

Working doc (plain markdown, md-only, never mirrored to `docs/`). Plans the deck series for Topic 10 "Doctrine of Creation vs Evolutionism" before any slides are written.

## Stance and Method

- **Diamond reading of Genesis 1–11.** The historical/literal, literary (structure, poetry, parallelism), theological/polemic, and moral (Sabbath pattern) facets are all true at once. The historical six-day timeline is one facet of the diamond – not in competition with the others.
- **What is rejected:** readings that must deny the historical timeline, and concordism – force-fitting modern science into Genesis 1 (day-age is the main case).
- **Two tests for every interpretation:**
  - (T1) Exegesis: does it come out of the text, as the author and his first audience would have read it?
  - (T2) Compatibility: can it stand alongside the historical timeline, or must it replace it?
- **Division of sources:**
  - (a) What the text says and which readings make hermeneutical sense – Craig (Series 4 Excursus 1–21) is the main source.
  - (b) How the text relates to science – CMI / AiG / ICR / DI are the main sources (`Sources/`), Craig Excursus 22–30 as the counterpart, RTB and BioLogos as opposing views to steelman.
- **Deck modes** (root.ai.md): the Genesis deck is mode (a) integration throughout, in a facet-cutting layout – the Diamond is stated first, then each view is examined for the facet it adds and polished (its flaws cut away). The science decks are evidence decks: state the claim, give the evidence, answer the rival.

### Deck 2 – The Days of Genesis

One big deck in the **facet-cutting pattern**: the Diamond comes first, as the thesis. Each interpretation is then a rough stone examined for the true facet it adds, and polished – its flaws cut away, only the shining part kept. The diamond is complete when every view has been cut. The Scripture is quoted once, before the cutting starts; the view sections refer back to it.

- **Part 1 – The Diamond** (the thesis, stated up front)
  - The atonement diamond as the familiar precedent (Christ Decks 3–6): several true motifs held together, none cancelling another.
  - Genesis 1–11 likewise: the historical 6 × 24h timeline is the core, and the literary, purposive, polemic, and moral (Sabbath) facets are all true at the same time.
  - What gets cut: readings that must deny the timeline, and concordism – science read into the text.
- **Part 2 – How to read Genesis**
  - Exegesis vs eisegesis vs concordism; genre; "when the plain sense makes common sense" (Series2.9.Summary).
  - The two cutting tools: (T1) does it come from the text? (T2) can it stand alongside the historical timeline?
  - **Every figure has a referent** (the third cutting tool): a figurative meaning rides on something concrete – calling a feature figurative does not remove the thing it refers to. Aquinas: all senses of Scripture are "founded on the literal" (*Summa Theologiae* I, q.1, a.10); Augustine's *signum* and *res*. Two kinds:
    - **Figurative description of a real event** – the words are figurative, the event is real: "God walked in the garden" → real daily closeness and conversation between God and Adam in a real place; "formed from dust" → a body really made of the earth ("to dust you shall return", Genesis 3:19).
    - **A real thing given a figurative meaning** (typology) – the thing itself is the referent: the two trees (a moral law carried by a concrete prohibition), the rib ("bone of my bones", Genesis 2:23), the serpent (Revelation 12:9), the cherubim, the six days (Exodus 20:11 needs a real week behind the pattern).
    - Even pure metaphors have referents: "the LORD is my rock" needs a real rock – big, heavy, not moved by wind, rain, or an angry man, a foundation to build on (Matthew 7:24–25) – whose solidity and reliability God truly has. No exceptions.
    - What differs is where the vehicle is: in a psalm the rock is out in the world and no event with a rock is narrated; in Genesis 2–3 the trees, the serpent, and the days are placed *inside* the story. When the vehicle is an object or event of the narrative, the narrative claims it existed.
  - **Map of the views:** the GenesisInterpretations chart (`docs/Creation/pics/GenesisInterpretations.svg`, generated by `tools/genesis-interpretations-chart.py`) – the roadmap for Part 4.
- **Part 3 – The text, once** (each passage quoted only here; later slides refer back):
  - Genesis 1:1–2:3 (yom with ordinals, evening-morning, day 4 light, day 7 without evening)
  - Genesis 2:4 (yom broadly)
  - Exodus 20:8–11 and 31:17
  - Mark 10:6
  - Genesis 1:29–30 and 9:3 (plant food)
  - Genesis 2:17, 3:17–19, Romans 5:12, 8:20–22 (death and thorns after the fall)
  - Genesis 5 and 11 (genealogies)
  - Genesis 7:19–23 (the Flood as text – the geology is in Deck 4)
- **Part 4 – Cutting the facets**, one section per view, in the chart's order. Each section has two parts:
  - **"X: The Facet"** – the view explained fairly (steelmanned), its strengths, and the facet it adds to the diamond.
  - **"X: Polishing the Facet"** – its weaknesses (T1/T2, referring back to Part 3), cut away; what shines is kept.

  | View | Facet kept | Cut away |
  |------|-----------|----------|
  | Literal accurate – YEC (Series2.9.Summary) | the historical timeline – the core | nothing essential; only over-reach (e.g. claiming the text teaches modern science) |
  | Literal naive (obsolete science) | the author meant ordinary days | "Moses described it with obsolete science" – the text is God's account |
  | Verse 1–2 gap (Chalmers, Scofield) | Genesis 1:2 describes a real first state – formless and empty | a pre-Adamic world, death and a fallen Satan before "very good" |
  | Day-gap (Lennox) | real days of divine action | ages inserted between the days, which the text does not mark |
  | Day-age (Ross / RTB; Hugh Ross review, Day-Age questions; FatManSpandexYomDay1, day-age timelines) | a real sequence of creative acts | yom stretched to ages – concordism; death before the fall |
  | Revelation-day / days of divine proclamation | God revealed the account – Moses was not there | the days as days of telling instead of days of making |
  | Literary framework (Blocher, Kline) | structure: forming (days 1–3) and filling (days 4–6) | structure *instead of* sequence |
  | Functional / cosmic temple (Walton) | purpose: creation as God's temple, ordered for function | function *instead of* material origins |
  | Creation myth (Miller and Soden; Craig Excursus 9–12) | polemic: Genesis answers the ANE myths | myth *instead of* history; no historical Adam |
  | Mytho-history (Craig Excursus 13–20) | the figurative layer – anthropomorphisms, the trees, the rib, the days carry real meaning; genealogies anchor the history | the referents: events treated as figurative because they seem "palpably false" (Excursus 19–20) – but the trees (Revelation 2:7, 22:2), serpent (Revelation 12:9), cherubim (Exodus 25, Ezekiel 1, 10), rainless Earth (the mist, Genesis 2:6) and days are real; Genesis 2 zooms in on day 6 rather than contradicting Genesis 1; "fantastic" means "miraculous", and the Creator makes a real tree of life no harder than a real Adam – concordism in reverse. The closest rival to the diamond |

- **Part 5 – The finished diamond**
  - Adam in the New Testament – Jesus and Paul read Genesis as history (Craig Man 12–14; CMI).
  - The polished diamond: the historical core plus every kept facet, on one slide – the thesis of Part 1, now earned.
  - Central truths of Genesis 1–11 (Craig Excursus 21) – common ground with other Christians.
- **Takeaways** – how to talk with old-earth Christians charitably: affirm the facet they see before showing what is cut.
