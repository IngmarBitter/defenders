# The Resurrection: Defeating the Alternatives --- ## Outline * [Two Ways to Know](#slide3) * [A History of Skepticism](#slide9) * [Three Facts to Be Explained](#slide17) * [Weighing the Hypotheses](#slide26) * [The Best Explanation](#slide36) * [Practical Takeaways](#slide42) --- ## Where We Are: From Evidence to Explanation ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

"And if Christ is not risen, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins!"

1 Corinthians 15:17 (NKJV)

* Last deck: the **biblical evidence** — the early creed, the witness list, the empty tomb, the transformed disciples * This deck: the **historical apologetic** — does "God raised Jesus" best explain that evidence? * The method: first establish the facts, then ask what best explains them --- # Two Ways to Know --- ## Existential and Evidential ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Two routes to knowing the resurrection is real: * **Existential** — a personal encounter with the living Lord; how most Christians have known it, a "properly basic" belief grounded in the Spirit's witness * **Evidential** — historical investigation of what actually happened * Both are valid; this deck walks the **evidential** road * The evidence can confirm and commend faith, but it is not the foundation that holds the simplest believer --- ## Two Presuppositions ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The historical argument openly assumes two things, each argued elsewhere: * **God exists** — established by natural theology (cosmological, teleological, moral arguments). Without this, miracles look impossible before the evidence is even weighed * **A core historical Jesus** — His life, His radical personal claims, and His death by crucifixion * These are mainstream conclusions of New Testament scholarship, not conservative assumptions * Whether you grant them dramatically shapes how you weigh the evidence --- ## The Two-Step Method ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Every historical argument for the resurrection has two stages: * **Step 1 — Establish the facts** that need explaining: the burial, the empty tomb, the appearances, the origin of the disciples' belief * **Step 2 — Infer the best explanation** of those facts * Keep them distinct. The crucial point: the **facts of Step 1 are not themselves miraculous** — only the explanation in Step 2 is --- ## Ehrman's Objection ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Bart Ehrman: there can be *in principle* **no historical evidence** for the resurrection * Why? The historian has access only to the natural world, never the supernatural. He can confirm that a *belief* arose, never that a miracle occurred * "It is a historical fact that some of Jesus' followers came to believe that he had been raised from the dead soon after his execution" — but the truth of that belief, he says, lies outside history --- ## Why the Objection Fails ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Even if granted, it strikes only at **Step 2** — the Step 1 facts (an empty grave, experiences of seeing Jesus) are not supernatural and are open to any historian * Ehrman himself, in his Teaching Company lectures, granted the burial, the empty tomb, and the appearances were historical — then quietly walked it back without refuting his own arguments * Historians and scientists routinely infer unseen entities — **dinosaurs, quarks** — as the best explanation, with no direct access * And why should a mere *methodological* rule stop us, as reasoning people, from following the evidence to a miracle? --- # A History of Skepticism --- ## The Enlightenment and Deism ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * For over 1,500 years the church read the resurrection as a literal event * The **Enlightenment** (17th–18th c.) threw off monarchy and church in the name of human reason and autonomy * Most theorists were not atheists but **deists**: a Creator and moral lawgiver, but no miracles, no special revelation, no inspired Scripture * Against that backdrop, modern skepticism about the resurrection arose — it comes in four families --- ## Reimarus: The Conspiracy Theory ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Hermann Samuel Reimarus (d. 1768): the disciples **stole the body** and faked the resurrection to keep the movement — and the easy life — going * To hold it he had to dismantle three things: the guard at the tomb, the apostles' testimony, and the prophecies * This was the **very first** counter-explanation — already on the scene in Matthew 28:11–15 — revived by the Enlightenment * Today universally abandoned by scholars --- ## Paulus: The Apparent Death Theory ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Heinrich Paulus (1828) and the "natural explanation" school: Jesus never died — He **swooned**, revived in the cool tomb, and escaped to convince the disciples * An advance on conspiracy: it grants the disciples' obvious **sincerity** * Even Friedrich Schleiermacher, the "father of modern theology," held it — a sobering fact --- ## Strauss: Myth, and the A Priori Against Miracles ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * David Friedrich Strauss (1835) buried both conspiracy and swoon as implausible * His alternative: **myth** — the Gospel accounts are legendary accretion. "External facts" were reduced to "inner events" * Decisively, he ruled the resurrection out **a priori**: a miracle is *by definition* unhistorical * Rudolf Bultmann in the 20th century is essentially the same — "demythologize" to find the spiritual kernel --- ## Lüdemann: Subjective Visions ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Gerd Lüdemann: the appearances were **hallucinations** (subjective visions); the empty tomb a late legend * His distinctive move is a **psychoanalysis**: Peter's guilt over his denial and Paul's guilt under the law (reading Romans 7) projected visions of Jesus * The whole theory takes **Paul's Damascus Road** experience as the template for every appearance --- ## Grass and Pannenberg: Objective Visions ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Hans Grass: the appearances were not mere projections but **objective visions** — God-given, like Stephen's vision of the Son of Man * Wolfhart Pannenberg (Craig's own doctoral supervisor) agrees — *and* affirms the historicity of the empty tomb * But why stop short of a bodily resurrection? Because modern theology will allow "psychological" miracles but not **nature** miracles --- ## Marxsen and Spong: The Interpretation Theory ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Willi Marxsen and Bishop John Shelby Spong: *something* powerful transformed the disciples — but we cannot say what * "Resurrection" was merely the **interpretive category** they reached for, and shouldn't be taken literally * Spong himself lists the radical changes — Sunday worship, the Trinity, Jesus as the message, enemies converted — that "cry out for an adequate explanation," then declines to give one --- # Three Facts to Be Explained --- ## Fact 1: The Empty Tomb — Burial and Site ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * If Joseph of Arimathea's **burial** is historical (see the previous deck), the tomb's location was publicly known * The proclamation "He is risen" could never have survived in Jerusalem with an occupied tomb a short walk away * This is why even the mundane burial account is a linchpin — and why skeptics must deny it on no good grounds --- ## The Empty Tomb — Multiple Early Sources ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The empty tomb is **early and multiply attested**: the pre-Markan passion source, the pre-Pauline creed (implicitly), plus independent strands in Matthew, Luke, John, and the sermons of Acts * The narrative is strikingly **simple** — no Scripture citations, no description of Jesus emerging in glory * Contrast the later forgery, the *Gospel of Peter*, where Jesus strides out of the tomb sky-high — that is what legend looks like --- ## The Empty Tomb — Women and Jewish Polemic ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The **women** as discoverers is an authenticity marker: their testimony carried little legal weight, so no one fabricating a story would cast them as the first witnesses * The Jewish counter-charge — "His disciples stole the body" (Matthew 28) — is **hostile testimony that presupposes an empty tomb** * Both sides agreed the tomb was empty; they only disputed *why* --- ## Fact 2: The Post-Mortem Appearances ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Paul's list (1 Corinthians 15:3–8) guarantees the experiences occurred: early, from a known author, inviting readers to **check the living witnesses** (see the previous deck) * To Peter, the Twelve, the five hundred, **James** (the skeptic brother), all the apostles, and Paul himself * The Gospels independently attest a wide range of appearances — too broad to be a late invention --- ## The Appearances Were Physical ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Paul's resurrection body is **physical** — transformed, not immaterial (see the previous deck) * The Gospels unanimously portray **bodily** appearances: eating, being touched, showing wounds * An "anti-Docetic invention" is unpersuasive — the diversity and physicality resist a vision-only origin --- ## Fact 3: The Origin of the Disciples' Belief ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * A **crucified Messiah** was a contradiction in terms — crucifixion should have shattered every messianic hope * Yet within days the disciples proclaimed a risen, vindicated Messiah and staked their lives on it * R. H. Fuller: even the most skeptical critic must posit "some mysterious X to get the movement going" * What was that X? --- ## Not from Christianity, Paganism, or Judaism ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The belief cannot come from **Christianity** — it did not yet exist; this belief is its foundation * Not from **paganism** — the alleged parallels are spurious, and such cults had no foothold in first-century Jewish Palestine * Not from **Judaism** — as the next slide shows, Jewish categories actively *resist* it * Left to themselves, the disciples would never have invented it --- ## The Jewish Categories: Translation vs Resurrection ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Jewish resurrection was always (1) at the **end of the world** and (2) of **all the righteous together** — never one isolated individual within history (Isaiah 26:19; Ezekiel 37; Daniel 12:2) * There was no concept of a **dying-and-rising Messiah** — N. T. Wright: across two centuries, no other movement said its executed leader had been raised * Crucial: a vision of a dead Jesus would mean **translation to heaven** (like Enoch and Elijah), *not* resurrection. They are different categories — and the disciples proclaimed the unexpected one --- # Weighing the Hypotheses --- ## McCullagh's Criteria ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Historian C. B. McCullagh: the best explanation of any body of facts is judged by — * **Explanatory scope** — does it cover the full range of data? * **Explanatory power** — does it make the data probable? * **Plausibility** — does it fit what else we know? * **Not *ad hoc*** — how many unsupported new assumptions must it add? * **Accord with accepted beliefs** * **Exceeds its rivals** on the conditions above * Now run each theory — then the resurrection — through this grid --- ## Conspiracy Hypothesis ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Adequate scope, but it fails on **power**: no fabricator would invent women witnesses, or so plain a tomb story, or omit appearances to the Sanhedrin * It is fatally **implausible** and anachronistic — first-century Jews had no category for faking a single man's resurrection * Worst of all, it **denies** rather than explains the disciples' sincere belief — the one fact nearly all scholars grant. This alone sank it --- ## Apparent Death Hypothesis ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * A scourged, crucified, speared man, sealed in a tomb, could not roll back the stone and escape — let alone appear as the **conqueror of death** * Strauss's decisive point: a half-dead Jesus in desperate need of bandaging would have evoked pity, not Easter faith * **Disconfirmed** by the medical facts of crucifixion; essentially no defenders today --- ## Wrong Tomb and Displaced Body ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * **Wrong tomb** (Kirsopp Lake): the women erred in the dark — but the site was known, it explains none of the appearances, and it arbitrarily keeps "He is not here" while dropping "He is risen" * **Displaced body** (Klausner): Joseph reburied the corpse — but he and his servants could simply have said so; Jewish law and practice argue against any such move * Both have **narrow scope** and collapse once the burial site is public knowledge --- ## Myth and Legend ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Strauss and Bultmann: the empty tomb and appearances are legend, the belief a later mythical development * But the resurrection belief is **primitive, not late** — the creed dates within about five years of the cross (previous deck) * And, as we saw, it cannot be sourced to Christian, pagan, or Jewish influences * There was no time, and no soil, for legend to grow this --- ## No Pagan Parallels ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The "dying-and-rising god" parallels turn out to be spurious: **apotheosis** stories (Hercules, Romulus), **disappearance** stories, or **seasonal** crop-cycle myths (Osiris, Adonis, Tammuz) — none a bodily resurrection within history * T. N. D. Mettinger, who defends a *minority* view that a few such gods existed, concedes Jesus' resurrection "retains its unique character... The riddle remains" * And there is **no causal link**: these cults were absent from first-century Palestine and abhorrent to Jews --- ## Hallucination Hypothesis — Narrow Scope ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * It addresses (at most) the appearances — and says **nothing** about the empty tomb, so it must deny the tomb or bolt on a second theory * It does not explain the **belief**: a vision of a dead man meant translation, not resurrection. James Dunn: "Why draw the astonishing conclusion that the eschatological resurrection had already taken place in the case of a single individual?" * The appearances' diversity — individuals and **groups**, believers and skeptics (James), even enemies (Paul) — bursts the psychological case-books. There are no group hallucinations --- ## Hallucination — Implausible Psychoanalysis ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Lüdemann's guilt-complex analysis rests on disputed **Freud/Jung** theory and on historical figures who cannot be examined — historians reject such psychobiography * Krister Stendahl: Paul the Pharisee was, by his own account, "blameless," with no guilt-ridden conscience — Romans 7 is **not** his pre-Christian autobiography (rejected by almost all expositors) * Paul includes himself in the witness list to **raise** his experience to the others' objectivity, not to drag theirs down to a vision --- ## Even Granting Visions: Translation, Not Resurrection ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Suppose, generously, that someone emptied the tomb and the shocked disciples then **hallucinated** Jesus * Hallucinations contain only what is already in the mind — so first-century Jewish minds would have seen Jesus **translated to heaven** (cf. *Testament of Job* 40), glorified, awaiting the last day * Translation is **not** resurrection. Even on the skeptic's best case, the origin of the resurrection belief stays **unexplained** --- # The Best Explanation --- ## The Resurrection Hypothesis: Scope and Power ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * "God raised Jesus from the dead" explains **all three** facts at once — the empty tomb, the appearances, and the origin of the belief * Its **explanatory power** is its great strength: on this hypothesis, the data are exactly what we would expect * Every rival explains only one or two of the facts, and renders the data improbable --- ## Plausible in Context ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Plausibility rises sharply in context — the arguments of **natural theology** (a good Creator God) plus Jesus' **unparalleled life and claims to divinity** * Once the philosophical prejudice against miracles is set aside, it is not at all implausible that God would raise *this* man * A resurrection fits Jesus of Nazareth as it would fit no one else --- ## Is It Ad Hoc? ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * McCullagh grants the scope and power but calls the hypothesis *ad hoc* * Yet it adds essentially **one** new supposition — that God exists — and for the theist that is already part of the background knowledge, not an extra assumption * The rivals smuggle in *more* unsupported assumptions: morally defective disciples, a merely superficial spear-thrust, a chain of psychological priming * Science freely posits new entities (quarks, black holes) without being called *ad hoc* --- ## A Miracle With a Context ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * A miracle *without* a context is ambiguous — it might be a mere anomaly or freak of nature * But this miracle is the **climax of Jesus' own life, ministry, and personal claims** — it fits "like a hand in the glove" * The truly contrived explanations are the rivals': that normal men became martyred liars, or that a dying man survived Roman crucifixion and exposure --- ## The Verdict ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * On every one of McCullagh's criteria, the resurrection hypothesis **meets or exceeds** its rivals * Scholars who reject it are, by and large, left with **no explanation at all** — the facts simply stand there unexplained * Once the prejudice against miracles is surrendered, "God raised Jesus from the dead" is the best explanation of the evidence --- # Practical Takeaways --- ## 1. Vindicates Christ's Person and Work ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * **His person:** God raised the very man executed for "blasphemy" — a dramatic divine endorsement of Jesus' claims to stand in God's place (Pannenberg) * **His work:** the resurrection shows the Father **accepted** the cross — divine justice fully satisfied, the penalty paid * Like a prisoner who has served his sentence and *must* be released, Christ having paid sin's penalty could not be held by death --- ## 2. Relationship with the Living Lord ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Because Jesus is alive, we can know Him **now** — union with the living Christ is the very essence of salvation (1 John 5:11–12) * This is the existential road of the opening slide: *"You ask me how I know He lives; He lives within my heart"* * The simplest believer, with no access to the evidence, can be **sure** — the witness of the living Lord holds constant in every generation --- ## 3. Hope in the Face of Death ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

"Jesus said to her, 'I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live. And whoever lives and believes in Me shall never die. Do you believe this?'"

John 11:25–26 (NKJV)

* Jesus speaks of death as one who has **conquered** it * The grave is not the end — and that gives our present choices eternal weight --- ## 4. Physical and Psychological Healing ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Christ is the **firstfruits** (1 Corinthians 15:20) — His resurrection body is the model of ours (Philippians 3:21) * At history's end: **glorious, immortal, powerful** bodies, free of disease, aging, and death (1 Corinthians 15:51–57) * And a **new psyche** — complete healing of the emotional scars every one of us carries --- ## 5. Guarantee of His Return ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The resurrection makes the Incarnation **permanent** — the Son is embodied forever, not only for His earthly sojourn * So the second coming cannot be demythologized away: He will return **bodily** and in glory (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17) * The same Jesus, in the same risen body, will come again --- ## How to Share It ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Remember the **two steps**: first the facts, then the best explanation * Memorize the **three facts** — empty tomb, appearances, origin of belief — they summarize all the evidence * This is *not* a "minimal facts" approach (Habermas) — it is a convenient summary; yet all three are in fact the **majority view** of New Testament scholars * Then keep hammering one question: what best explains all three? --- ## Credits ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * **Ingmar Bitter**, slides content * **William Lane Craig**, *Defenders 3: Doctrine of Christ*, Parts 33–46, primary theological source * Full notes: https://www.reasonablefaith.org/podcasts/defenders-podcast-series-4/doctrine-of-christ * **C.
