**Tactics** Be Winsome to Win Some
Ingmar Bitter, PhD --- ## Outline * [Why Tactics](#slide3) * [A Conversation That Works](#slide6) * [Summary](#slide11) --- ## Status of This Deck * **Preview only.** This deck is a placeholder, holding material that came up while teaching the Doctrine of Christ before the Tactics unit is written * Primary source when the unit is built: Gregory Koukl, *Tactics: A Game Plan for Discussing Your Christian Convictions* * Everything here is expected to be reworked, expanded, and reordered --- # Why Tactics --- ## The Goal Is a Conversation, Not a Verdict * Our aim is not to win the exchange but to **put a stone in the shoe** – leave something they keep walking on * "Always be ready to give a defense... with **meekness and fear**" (1 Peter 3:15) – the manner is part of the command * Do not open by telling someone they are wrong. Every worldview says the others are wrong; the question is whether you will give **reasons** * Questions do the work that assertions cannot: they let a person hear his own view out loud --- ## The Offense Is Not That We Are Different * People rarely object that Christianity is *different*; they object that it says other views are **wrong** * But every worldview does that, implicitly. Pluralism itself says the particularists are wrong * So the disagreement is not the scandal – it is the price of believing anything at all * What is owed is not silence but a reason --- # A Conversation That Works --- ## Start Where They Are: "What Does It Take?" * Open with a question, not a claim: **"What do you think it takes to go to heaven?"** * The answer tells you which conversation you are actually in: * A works answer – go to the moral law * "There is no heaven" – go to the world before you go to the Bible * A Christian answer – ask what makes them sure --- ## If There Is No Heaven: Start with the World * Move to what physics does not explain: why there is a universe at all, and why this one * The universe has not always been here; it **came into being** – and things that begin have causes * This is not yet the Gospel. It is only a doorway: something is there that the materialist account does not reach * Let the question sit rather than pressing for a concession --- ## Then the Moral Law: Make It Personal * Move from the universe to the conscience – from an abstract God to **their** standing before Him * The Ten Commandments, then the Sermon on the Mount: lust is adultery, hatred is murder (Matthew 5:21–22, 27–28) * The standard is not "better than most" but the heart, and nobody clears it * The aim is not to shame but to let the self-made case for one's own goodness **collapse on its own** --- ## Then Ask What They Actually Believe * Once their view has given way, do not rush to fill the silence – ask what they think *is* true * Only now is the answer about Christ an answer to a question they are asking * Every other founder is dead and buried; the **resurrection** is what sets the claim apart * Leave the door open: a conversation that ends well can be continued --- # Summary --- ## The Shape of It * Ask before you assert; the question is the tactic * Go world – conscience – Christ, in that order, when there is no shared starting point * Let their own view do the arguing against itself * Aim at the next conversation, not at a surrender * Gentleness and respect are not softening of the message; they are part of it --- ## Credits * **Ingmar Bitter**, slides content * **Gregory Koukl**, *Tactics: A Game Plan for Discussing Your Christian Convictions* – the primary source for this unit when it is built out * Material captured from the Doctrine of Christ classes, where these questions kept coming up in practice --- ## Navigation * [Creekside Defenders Main Page](http://tinyurl.com/CreeksideDefenders) ---
