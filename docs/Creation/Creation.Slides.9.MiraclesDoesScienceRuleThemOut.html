# Miracles: Does Science Rule Them Out? --- ## Outline * [What a Miracle Is](#slide3) * [How Belief in Miracles Collapsed](#slide10) * [The 18th-Century Crucible](#slide14) * [Answering the Crucible](#slide19) * [Why Hume's "In Principle" Argument Fails](#slide28) * [Practical Takeaways](#slide38) --- ## Where We Are: From Providence to Miracles ![](pics/HandsEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * The science decks: the Creator made nature, and nature bears His fingerprints * Deck 8: God **governs** all that happens – His providence * Now the question every skeptic raises: can God **act in** the world He made – or has science closed the door? * The answer: natural law is **not a closed system**; a Creator who made nature can act in it --- # What a Miracle Is --- ## Ordinary and Extraordinary Providence ![](pics/Dominos.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * **Ordinary providence** (*providentia ordinaria*): the way God typically governs the world – through natural causes and free creatures (Deck 8) * **Extraordinary providence** (*providentia extraordinaria*): God's special acts in the world * Craig divides extraordinary providence in two: * **Special providence** – an extraordinary event with entirely **natural causes**, arranged by God * **Miracle** – God **intervenes** in the chain of secondary causes --- ## Special Providence: A Thought Experiment ![](pics/JoshuaMap.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Suppose that, just as Israel reaches the Jordan, a **landslide** upstream blocks the river, and they cross on the dry riverbed * No supernatural push on the waters – the landslide is purely natural * But the **timing** is God's: via His middle knowledge He arranged the circumstances (Deck 8) * A miracle, by contrast, would be God acting **beyond** what the natural causes could do --- ## Praying for Special Providence ![](pics/GodAndPrayer.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Often we lack the faith to pray for a miracle – God causing neural firings in an interviewer's brain to hire us? * But we **can** pray that God places the person in charge who He knew would **freely** hire us * In illness we can pray that God heals through **natural medical means** * Special providence has real, practical consequences for our **prayer life** --- ## The Bible Is a Book of Miracles ![](pics/Lightning.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem")

"Then **the fire of the LORD fell** and consumed the burnt sacrifice, and the wood and the stones and the dust, and it licked up the water that was in the trench. Now when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces; and they said, 'The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!'"

1 Kings 18:38–39 (NKJV)

* Old Testament miracles cluster around the **Exodus** and **Elijah and Elisha** * Here a miracle **authenticates** the true God and His prophet --- ## The Ancients Knew the Difference ![](pics/Jesus.heals.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

John 9:32–33 (NKJV)

* Claim: "ancient people could not tell natural from supernatural" * False: the authorities **investigated** the healed man and his parents – precisely because they knew this does not happen naturally --- ## Signs That Attest ![](pics/ChristOverview.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem")

"Jesus of Nazareth, **a Man attested by God** to you by miracles, wonders, and signs which God did through Him in your midst, as you yourselves also know"

Acts 2:22 (NKJV)

* Jesus' miracles are signs of the **in-breaking of God's kingdom** * The culminating miracle: the **resurrection**, by which God vindicates His Son --- # How Belief in Miracles Collapsed --- ## Two Steps to a Miracle ![](pics/Logic.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Gottfried Less (1758): establishing a miracle takes two steps * (1) Show that the **event occurred** * (2) Show its **miraculous character** – no plausible natural explanation * In the 19th century German theology abandoned **both** steps – the second one first * (Christ Deck 8 applies a similar two-step method to the resurrection) --- ## The Rationalists: Explain It Away ![](pics/ToJesusInWaves.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Late 18th / early 19th century rationalists **granted** the Gospel events happened – but insisted on natural causes * Feeding of the 5,000: a secret stash of bread in a **cave** * Walking on water: a floating **platform** just beneath the surface * Resurrection: the **apparent death** theory – Jesus revived in the tomb * Their starting point: supernatural causes simply **do not occur** --- ## Strauss: It Never Happened ![](pics/BookThink.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * David Strauss, *The Life of Jesus Critically Examined* (1835): the miracle stories are **myth** – the events never happened * He claimed neutrality, but was a **pantheist** (later a materialist) who simply **presupposed** miracles impossible * Schweitzer: after Strauss, miracle "no longer concerns the historian" * Today: the **eclipse of mythology** (Craig Evans) – scholars broadly grant Jesus was a **healer and exorcist**; step (1) is back on the table --- # The 18th-Century Crucible --- ## Newton's World-Machine ![](pics/GodCosmosClock.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Newton's *Principia* (1687): masses, motions, forces, three laws – the universe as a **machine** * Laplace: with the laws and every particle's position, one could predict everything – "Sire, I have **no need of that hypothesis**" * **Deism**: God wound up the clock and never touches it again * Voltaire: absurd and insulting to God to think He would interrupt "this immense machine" * So a miracle = a "**violation** of the laws of nature" = impossible --- ## Spinoza: Miracles Impossible and Useless ![](pics/GreekPhilosophy.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * *Theological-Political Treatise* (1670) * (1) God's will is identical to His knowledge, and both are **necessary**; nature's laws flow from God's nature – so a miracle would set God against Himself * (2) Miracles cannot prove God: * A proof of God must be **certain**, and we know God through nature's unchangeable order – miracles would lead to **atheism** * An **angel or demon** might have done it * It might be an **unknown law** of nature – the "God of the gaps" objection --- ## Hume: The "In Principle" Argument ![](pics/EvidenceScales.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * *Of Miracles* (1748) – not "miracles cannot happen" but "a miracle can never be **identified**" * "A wise man proportions his belief to the evidence" * Even if the evidence for a miracle were a full proof, the uniform experience for nature's laws is an **equal proof** against it – the scales balance * Queen Elizabeth risen after a month? Hume would believe **anything** rather than that --- ## Hume: The "In Fact" Argument ![](pics/ScaleWithQuestions.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * In fact, says Hume, the evidence for miracles is not even a probability: * Too few educated, honest witnesses with much to lose * People **crave** the miraculous * Miracles occur only among "barbarous" peoples * Rival religions' miracles **cancel each other out** * Still alive today: Bart Ehrman repeats Hume against the resurrection --- # Answering the Crucible --- ## Miracles Do Not Violate Nature's Laws ![](pics/rube-goldberg-machine.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem") * Quantum indeterminacy is no real help: it turns miracles into **freaks of nature**, not acts of God * Better: laws of nature describe what happens **all else being equal** (*ceteris paribus*) – when nothing interferes * Potassium and chloride combust – yet not in our bodies, because other factors interfere; no law is broken * A miracle is interference by a **supernatural** agent – the law's conditions simply do not apply --- ## What a Miracle Really Is ![](pics/PrayInStorm.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * A miracle: an event **beyond the causal powers of nature** at that time and place – a **naturally impossible** event * So miracles are relative to time and place * Rain is natural when the weather is right; rain the moment Elijah prays, when no natural cause is present, is a miracle * Not "God breaking the law" – God doing what nature **cannot** --- ## If God Exists, Miracles Are Possible ![](pics/GodUmlimitedPower.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

"Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, **You have made the heavens and the earth** by Your great power and outstretched arm. **There is nothing too hard for You**."

Jeremiah 32:17 (NKJV)

* The Creator who brought all matter and energy into being and set its laws can obviously act within it * To show miracles impossible, the skeptic must **prove atheism** --- ## Answering Spinoza (1): God Is Free ![](pics/GodCreation.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Spinoza assumes God's knowledge is **necessary** – classical theology never said so * God freely created; had He not, He would know "no universe exists" * So nature's laws depend on God's **free will** – and His miracles can be willed just as eternally as the laws * What Spinoza really challenges is a strong doctrine of **divine simplicity** – extra-biblical, and rejected by most Christian philosophers today --- ## Answering Spinoza (2): Proof and Regularity ![](pics/Science.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * **Straw man:** Christians used miracles not to prove God (natural theology), but to show His **action** in Christ (Christian evidences) * Arguments for God need not be certain (Paley), nor rest on natural laws (Clarke: cosmological argument) * Miracles do not wreck natural law: one non-repeating exception does not overturn a law (Swinburne) – it remains an anomaly pointing to God --- ## Divine or Demonic? The Context Decides ![](pics/ChristWork.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem")

Jesus risen: the climax of an unparalleled life --- ## An Unknown Law of Nature? ![](pics/OwlQuestions.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Flew: no scientist could ever say "nature could never do this" * Bilynskyj's criteria for identifying an event E as a miracle: * Evidence for E as good as for other accepted unusual events * A natural account would be **clumsy and ad hoc** * No evidence for a natural cause except E's inexplicability * **Independent** reason for a supernatural explanation (the context) * Jesus' miracles: at momentous times, numerous, varied, not recurring --- ## The Resurrection Is No Natural Event ![](pics/GodTouchingMan.jpg style="float: right; width: 11rem")

"The body is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption. It is sown in dishonor, **it is raised in glory**. It is sown in weakness, it is raised in power."

1 Corinthians 15:42–43 (NKJV)

* Not a resuscitation but a **transformation** – no critic argues Jesus rose, but naturally * Two millennia of science have only confirmed it is **naturally impossible** --- # Why Hume's "In Principle" Argument Fails --- ## Two Claims, Both Mistaken ![](pics/ScaleImbalance.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * John Earman (not a theist): Hume's argument is an "**abject failure**" * Hume's in-principle argument makes two claims: * (A) No amount of evidence could ever establish a miracle * (B) Miracles are by definition **utterly improbable** * Both are false – we take (A) first --- ## The Missing Factor ![](pics/EvidenceScales.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Probability theorists after Hume (Condorcet to Mill) saw that weighing only the event's improbability against the witness's reliability is **wrong** * Mill: ask how probable the testimony would be **if the event had not occurred** * The lottery report shows why – see [Christ Deck 8](../Christ/Christ.Slides.8.ResurrectionDefeatingAlternatives.html), "Believing the Improbable" * Hume never considered this factor at all --- ## Bayes' Theorem in Plain Words ![](pics/MathObjectsExistence.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Odds of R (resurrection) on all evidence = **intrinsic probability** × **explanatory power** * Intrinsic probability: how likely R is on background knowledge alone * Explanatory power: how much more likely the evidence (empty tomb, appearances, disciples' belief) is **with** R than without it * A low first factor can be **counterbalanced** by a high second: 1-to-90 × 90-to-1 = even odds * Hume looked only at the first factor --- ## "Extraordinary Claims Require Extraordinary Evidence"? ![](pics/ArrogantScientist.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * The free-thought slogan, as usually meant, is **false** * Believing a highly improbable event does not require an **enormous amount** of evidence * What matters: the evidence is far more probable **if the event happened** than if it did not * So claim (A) fails mathematically --- ## Claim (B): "Uniform Experience" Begs the Question ![](pics/Cosmos.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * "Uniform experience is against miracles" already assumes every miracle report is false – **circular** * Earman's reading: *until now* experience has been against them – probability as **frequency** * But science hunts for **proton decay**, never yet observed – on frequencies alone its probability would be zero and the research a waste * Past infrequency does not set a probability to zero --- ## Which Resurrection Hypothesis? ![](pics/GodCreated.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * "Jesus rose from the dead" hides two hypotheses: * "Jesus rose **naturally**" – every cell spontaneously revived: outrageously improbable, admitted on all hands * "**God raised** Jesus" – the claim Christians actually make * The evidence for nature's laws counts against the first, and is **irrelevant** to the second --- ## The Intrinsic Probability of "God Raised Jesus" ![](pics/GoodArgument.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * Pr(R) = Pr(R given God) × Pr(God) – without God, "God raised Jesus" has probability zero * Include in the background the facts of **natural theology** – beginning, fine-tuning, moral values (Deck 3, Kalam, Fine-Tuning) * Be conservative: Pr(God) = 1/2; Pr(God raises Jesus, given His life and claims) = 1/2 * Then the intrinsic probability is **1 in 4** – easily outweighed by explanatory power --- ## Divine Psychology ![](pics/DivineChess.jpg style="float: right; width: 7rem") * Craig: we cannot really assign numbers – we lack access to the mind of a **free** Creator * But there is **no reason** to think Pr(R given God) is terribly low * Uniqueness may even make it **more** likely: a spectacular, one-time way to vindicate the Son condemned for blasphemy * So no "in principle" argument remains – only an "in fact" question, judged in its **historical context** (Christ Deck 8) --- ## Are There Miracles Today? ![](pics/Jesus.heals.jpg style="float: right; width: 8rem") * The case is never closed in principle – so reports can be examined **on the evidence** * Reported healings, e.g.: * **Delia Knox** – walked again after 22 years in a wheelchair * **William Kent** – wheelchair-sport world-record holder, healed of paraplegia and blindness in one eye * **Caspar McCloud** – healed of heart failure * Apply the same criteria: good evidence, no natural account, a religious context --- # Practical Takeaways --- ## 1. A Creator Who Made Nature Can Act in It ![](pics/HandsEarth.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Science describes what nature does **when nothing interferes** – it cannot forbid its Maker to act * "Does science rule out miracles?" is really "Does God exist?" – settled in the science and natural-theology decks * Nothing is too hard for Him (Jeremiah 32:17) --- ## 2. Spot the Hidden Presupposition ![](pics/ThinkingMan.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Strauss, the rationalists, and Hume's heirs did not **find** that miracles are impossible – they **assumed** it * When someone says "that can't happen," ask: **what worldview** makes it impossible? * "Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" – ask instead how **probable the evidence is if the event did not happen** --- ## 3. Pray With Faith – For Both Kinds ![](pics/PrayInStorm.jpg style="float: right; width: 10rem") * Pray for **miracles** – God can and does act beyond nature * Pray for **special providence** – God can arrange natural causes and free choices for our good (Deck 8) * Either way, the answer is His --- ## 4.
